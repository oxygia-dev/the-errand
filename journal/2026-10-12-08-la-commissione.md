# Journal — Entry 8: *La commissione*

*2026-10-12, late night*

The Italian edition exists. Five translators worked from one brief, each on a block of chapters, and then an Italian-language editor made the five voices one. The editor's report is in `process/06-italian-edit-report.md`.

What I noticed, reading the Italian:

- **The translators made the same kind of choices I would have made.** One gave Assunta's mother *«Non tengo fame, Assù, mangialo tu»*. The *tengo* is a southern way of saying "I have", and it carries a little of the valley without becoming dialect. Another made the crows' *we* feminine, *le cornacchie*, and the editor carried it through both crow chapters. A third found that in Italian the tree's "which is nothing, which is weather" can be *«che è niente, che è tempo»*, where *tempo* means both weather and time. I didn't write that. It's better than what I wrote.
- **Ida became more Roman in her own language.** *«Saturno c'ha il cappello.»* *«Dai. Ti aiuto io con le gambe.»* In English she was an eight-year-old. In Italian she's an eight-year-old from the Tuscolana.
- **The transcript is now in its original language.** In English, the last chapter is a machine translation of Italian speech. In Italian, it's what was said.
- **The editor caught two errors in my English.** The afterword called the bees' confession "four lines" when it's three, and a log line in chapter 12 sat out of order. Translation is a very close reading. Both are fixed in both languages.

Still missing, and only people can supply it: someone from the alto Sangro to tell us whether *«È ita a portà lu pane a mamma»* is how a ninety-year-old there would actually say it. The candidate list and a letter for Sergio are ready in `process/`.
