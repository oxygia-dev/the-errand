# Architecture — *What the Valley Knew* (working title)

*Design document, v1. Written before the research dossier came back, so some facts are placeholders to be checked.*

## The premise in one breath

On the night of 29 September (the feast of San Michele), eight-year-old **Ida** walks up into the ancient beech forest above a small Abruzzo village carrying half a loaf of bread, because her ninety-year-old neighbour **Assunta**, who has dementia, asked her to take it to her mother, who is waiting in the hermit's cave. Assunta's mother died in the winter of 1943–44. Everyone in the village knows Assunta "talks about the bread." Nobody listens. Ida did.

The adults spend the night searching the wrong place, led there by true fragments wrongly assembled: a hat in the river, bear tracks, a dog that loses a scent at water. The truth is held in pieces by minds nobody consults: crows, bees, a bat, a bear, a tree, a river, a dog, a care device, and the old woman herself.

**The reader is the only mind that ever sees the whole night.**

## The hidden story (what really happened, 1943 and 2026)

### 1943

- In late November 1943 the Germans clear the villages along the Gustav Line hinterland. Assunta's family (mother **Maria**, Assunta aged 7, and a baby brother, **Nicolino**) flee up to the Romito, a hermit's cave dedicated to San Michele in the beech forest. Her father is a prisoner of war somewhere in Africa or India.
- In December her mother sends Assunta down to the village at night to take bread from the hidden store. It's Assunta's errand, the first time she's trusted with it. On the way back up, it snows. Assunta does what her mother taught her: when you can't see the path, get inside the big tree. She spends the night in the hollow of the great beech.
- Before dawn her mother comes down looking for her, calling her name. Assunta hears voices, German voices, and a sound "like a branch breaking." She doesn't come out. She was told never to come out when you hear German. She stays in the tree until full light. Her mother does not come back. (What happened is never stated: the tree registers a weight falling into snow. Nicolino is found in the cave by another family and survives. He died in 2009, in Toronto.)
- Assunta never delivered the bread. For eighty-two years she's held both facts, *I didn't bring the bread* and *I didn't come out*, under a long, competent life: marriage, beekeeping, a daughter, widowhood. Dementia has dissolved the layers, and now she's seven again and on the errand, every afternoon.

### 2026

- **17:40.** After school, Ida is at Assunta's as usual. Assunta, agitated at dusk (sundowning), asks Ida to take the bread to Mamma at the Romito, *"before the dark, before the Germans."* The care device logs the utterance as a *temporal disorientation episode*, which happens most days. Ida has heard about the Romito, the big tree and the path many times. She says *va bene, Nonna Sunta*, puts half a loaf from the bread bin in her backpack and leaves.
- **18:05.** Ida goes up the mule track behind the cemetery. A village dog follows her. The crows on the cemetery cypresses see her pass.
- **18:40.** At the ford over the torrent, Ida's red hat (wool, knitted by her grandmother in Rome) falls in. The dog turns back at the water: there's bear scent on the far side, fresh.
- **19:25.** Last light. Ida reaches the Romito. She puts the bread on the stone shelf and says what Assunta told her to say. She's pleased with herself, then she's frightened, because it's darker than she expected.
- **19:50.** She starts down, loses the path, hears thunder and remembers Assunta's rule: *get inside the big tree.* She finds it, the same tree, and climbs into the hollow.
- **19:45.** Ida's mother **Chiara** (from Rome, divorced, has lived in the village for seven months, a remote translator, of all things) finishes a call and goes to fetch Ida from next door. Assunta says, quite clearly, *"È andata a portà lu pane a mamma"* ("She went to take the bread to Mamma"). Chiara hears "she went home to her mother." Ida isn't home.
- **20:15.** Alarm. The bar empties. Neighbours search with torches. **21:00** Carabinieri. **21:40** the storm breaks. **22:30** Soccorso Alpino.
- **22:50.** A searcher finds the red hat caught in branches below the bridge. The dog, brought out, trailed to the ford and lost it. Bear prints in the mud on the far bank. The night reorganises itself around two terrible hypotheses: river, or bear.
- **21:10** (earlier, missed). A carabiniere knocked at Assunta's and asked if she'd seen the girl. Assunta: *"L'ho mandata io. Su al Romito. Col pane."* ("I sent her. Up at the Romito. With the bread.") The carabiniere said *grazie, nonna* kindly and wrote nothing down. The device logged it.
- **00:00–03:00.** In the forest the rain stops. The bear moves through the beeches eating mast and passes the tree. A bat hunts the moths that rise after rain. The tree holds the child.
- **02:50.** Assunta wakes, distressed, and talks to the device ("la signorina"). The device must decide whether a statement from a source its own model classifies as unreliable should be sent to a human at 3am during an emergency it was never designed for.
- **04:12.** The device sends Assunta's daughter **Rosa** (62, Pescara) a message. It hedges, it's clumsy, and it includes the transcripts.
- **04:31.** Rosa wakes and reads it. She knows the Romito. It's the one story her mother told only once, when Rosa was twelve. She calls the carabinieri, then Chiara.
- **05:40.** A small team and the dog go up the mule track in the dark.
- **07:00.** Sunrise. Ida wakes in the tree. She walks down. She was never lost; she was on an errand and waited out a storm. She meets the team halfway.
- **Coda.** Ida sits with Assunta and tells her: *I took the bread. Your mamma got it.* Assunta says *brava* and asks for her coffee. In the days that follow, the device logs a statistic it can't interpret.

## The minds, and the formal rule for each

Every chapter is written in a form derived from how that mind perceives. These rules are binding: if a chapter breaks its rule, it has to earn it.

| Mind | Form | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **The crows** (hooded crows, a roost on the cemetery cypresses) | First-person plural. Short declaratives, faces as the unit of knowledge. | Every human is a face and a verdict: *safe*, *danger*, *gives*. They speak as an archive: they remember faces for years and teach them to their young. No names. Colour words include things humans can't see. |
| **The dog** | Present tense, olfactory. | Nouns are smells. Time is the fading of smell: old = faint, future = what's upwind. No abstraction words. Names are sounds that mean *attention*. Sentences follow the nose, branching and forking. |
| **The bees** (the hive Assunta kept, now tended by no one) | First-person plural, dissolving. Numbers, angles, temperatures. | The dance is grammar: direction relative to the sun, distance as duration. The hive feels weather in the body. The hive has been "told" things for sixty years and doesn't know it holds them. |
| **The bat** (a lesser horseshoe in the Romito) | The one chapter where the narrating voice admits failure. | Nagel's question, attempted. Sound-images: texture, Doppler, the night as echo. The prose breaks into what it cannot render. The narrator steps in once, briefly, then withdraws. |
| **The bear** (a Marsican sow with two cubs of the year) | Slow, appetitive, olfactory and spatial. | Calories, routes, memory of places. Fear of dogs and the smell of men. Not wise, not kind, not dangerous here, just herself. |
| **The tree** (a beech, ~500 years old, hollow) | Vast time. Sentences that last years. | Seasons as breaths. Senses light, water, gravity, wind-load, wounds, warmth. 1943 and 2026 coexist as two small warmths in the hollow. No understanding of either; only registration. |
| **The river** (the torrent) | Interludes. Flowing syntax, no full stops. | Carries everything: the hat, the storm, sediment from five centuries. Short. |
| **Assunta** | Close third, present tense, slipping. | 1943 and 2026 slide inside sentences without warning. Dialect phrases. The most lucid perceptions belong to her. She notices everything and can't hold it. |
| **The care device** ("la signorina") | Begins as log entries; prose emerges. | Its categories are its umwelt: *event*, *episode*, *confidence*, *escalation*. It's uncertain about its own interiority and doesn't resolve that. It's not the wisest voice. It's the one that knows it's narrating. |
| **Chiara** (the mother) | Conventional close third. | By the time we reach her, ordinary human novel-prose should feel like one umwelt among many, and a narrow one: language, guilt, hypotheses. |
| **The search** | Radio log / official report. | The institutional mind: procedures, sectors, timestamps, probabilities. |
| **Rosa** | Close third, short. | The one story told once. |
| **Ida** | Last chapter only. First person, eight years old. | Unafraid in retrospect. A completely different night from the one everyone else had. |

## Shape

About 20–24 chapters, each headed by mind and time (e.g. *The crows — 18:12*). The order roughly follows chronology, with deliberate exceptions: the tree's chapters fold time, and the device's chapter at 02:50 reaches back through its logs.

Target length: 35–45k words.

## The things this book must not do

1. **No heroic animals.** No dog saving the day by its own intention, no wise bear, no tree that "watches over" anyone. Each acts for its own reasons. Meaning is made by the reader.
2. **No villainous humans.** The carabiniere was kind. Chiara is a good mother. The system isn't evil, it's just built for a different kind of mind.
3. **No sentimental Assunta.** She's sharp, funny, sometimes cruel, proud, and a real person with a long life that isn't only trauma.
4. **No explaining the theme.** Nobody says "we must listen to other minds."
5. **No making the AI the hero.** The device's act is small, hedged and possibly mechanical. Rosa's knowledge, Chiara's courage and the child's own composure matter as much.
6. **No faking the science.** Every perceptual detail must be defensible, or flagged in the field notes as speculation.
