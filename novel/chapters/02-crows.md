## 2. The crows

We know the faces.

Every face that comes up the road we have already seen or we have not, and if we have not we watch it until we have, and then it is known, and what is known is told. That is the whole of it. That is what we are for.

The cypresses at the place of the stones are ours at dusk. We come in from the stubble and the river and the rubbish behind the bar, in twos and threes, and we sit in the black tops of the trees and we say what we saw. The light goes. We say it louder. This is not quarrelling, though it sounds like quarrelling to the faces below. It is the day being put away.

Here is what is known.

The face with the stick and the dog and the brown hat walks the road at first light and at last light and gives nothing and takes nothing. Neither.

The face with the long shining stick that went *crack* in the winter of the deep snow, from the house with the blue gate: danger. Most of us were not hatched in the winter of the deep snow. It does not matter. It was told to us. We tell it to the young ones, and when that face comes out of the blue gate we go up off the wires all at once, all of us, even the ones who never heard the crack, and we scream it down the street, *that one, that one*, and that face looks up at us and does not understand how it is known. It is known because we are many and we are long. One crow forgets. We do not.

The bent face from the house with the boxes that hum, the one who moves slowly and talks to herself: gives. Has given for longer than any of us has been alive. Scraps on the wall behind the boxes, every day, the yellow ends of the bread, the rind, the bones of the small fish. The ones before us knew her face, and the ones before them. Some days now she forgets. Some days she comes out to the wall with nothing in her hands and stands and talks to the boxes and goes in again. We watch. We wait. It is not hers to forget. But she is still gives.

The small face from the house beside her, new this winter: gives. Learned quickly. Crusts on the wall at the edge of the school-noise, when the big faces are not looking. Comes close. Too close. Looks at us. Looks at us the way we look at faces, which is not how faces look; faces look at the shape of us and away; this one looks at *us*, at this one and that one, and so we know she is learning us the way we learn her, and this is strange, and we have said so, many times, at dusk, and decided nothing.

Gives. Small. New. Looks.

---

Now the small face is on the road.

It is late for the small face. At this hour the small faces are inside, behind the yellow windows. We know the small faces' hours better than they do. But here she is, coming past the place of the stones, past the iron gate, with the bag on her back that shines in the colour the faces cannot see, so that even in the dusk, even among the white stones and the grey, she is bright to us as a fish in a stream.

The dog is with her. The dog is nuisance: too slow to be danger, too stupid to be anything else. The dog barks at us. We let it.

The small face does not go into the place of the stones, where faces go to put flowers and stand still. She goes on, past it, to where the road becomes the path, where the stones of the path are loose and the trees begin. The trees that go up and up into the dark that is not ours.

We are quiet for a moment. All of us. It moves through the cypresses: one stops, and the next, and the next, until we are just sitting, and the only sound is the dog.

The small face does not look back. She is walking quickly, the way faces walk when they have somewhere to be. One hand holds the straps of the bag. The other hand is at her head, at the red thing on her head, pulling it down, because the wind has come round from the mountain and is cold.

Then she is in the trees, and the trees close, and she is gone, and the dog is gone after her.

We start talking again. Louder.

---

This is what is known about the up-trees.

The up-trees are not for faces at night. Faces go into the up-trees in the day, a few at a time, with baskets, in the season of the wet brown smell when the mushrooms come, or with saws, or with the long shining sticks in the cold season, and then we stay away. At night the faces stay down in their yellow boxes and the up-trees belong to the others.

Once in each turning of the year, all the faces go up together. This morning was the once. The bell went mad, and the faces came out of every door at the same time, which they never do, and went up the road and past the place of the stones and up the path with the tall gold thing carried on their shoulders, swaying, and a noise of brass in front of them, and we went up off the cypresses all at once and followed them a little way, because when faces move like that, together, slowly, there is sometimes food after. There was food after. Down in the square, at noon, sugar and grease on the ground. The small face was in it. Holding the hand of the face that waters the red flowers. Neither. The small face had dust to the knees and was talking the whole way, up and back.

So: the small face has been up the path today already. In the light. With all the others.

This is not the same.

The big one is in the up-trees. The one who opens trees, who pulls down the branches of the plum trees behind the last house and sits in the orchard at grey-light with the purple running off her mouth, and the two small ones of her, round as stones, rolling. We follow the big one. We wait in the tops of the plum trees while she eats, and we take what she drops, and when she goes we take what is left. She does not care about us. She is not danger. She is not gives. She is the big one. She is a kind of weather.

The big one has been in the plum trees five grey-lights out of the last seven. She came down from the up-trees on the path the small face just went up.

This is known. It is not said. There is nothing to say about it. It is only a thing among the things.

---

Darkness. Nearly. The colour has gone out of everything except the sky behind the mountain, where it is still the colour of the inside of a mouth.

The last ones come in. The quarrelling settles. We shift, and grip, and lean against each other's shoulders in the black tops of the cypresses, and the grey of our backs goes the same grey as the air.

Below us the dog comes back.

Alone. Fast. Wet to the belly. Coming down the path and out of the trees with his tail low and his head low, past the iron gate, past the place of the stones, without stopping to bark at us, which he always does, which is the one thing he can be relied upon to do. He goes down the road toward the yellow windows and does not look up.

We watch him go.

The small face does not come back down the path.

We wait for it, a little. Not for long. We are not made for waiting in the dark; the dark is when we close. But some of us wait, a little, with one eye on the place where the trees closed. We do not know why. Nothing is owed. She is only gives. There are other faces that give.

But she looks at us. She is learning us. She knows this one and that one, she has a sound for the one with the white feather in his wing, she says it every time she sees him, the same sound. *Bianchino.* Like that. She is the only face that has ever had a sound for one of us.

We do not have a sound for her. We do not have sounds for faces. We have *gives* and *danger* and *neither*, and we have the face itself, which we keep, the way the stones keep the names cut into them.

We keep hers.

Under us, down in the yellow windows, something has begun. A door, and a voice, high, then another door. The voice says the same sound again and again, going up at the end each time like a question, like a call to a young one that has strayed.

We do not know the sound. It is not one of ours.

We tuck our heads under. The voice goes on. Somewhere up in the up-trees, the wind turns over, and the first of the cold comes down off the mountain smelling of stone and water and something coming, something big, from the far side of the ridge, the way weather comes.

We sleep with it. We will know in the morning. In the morning it will be told.
