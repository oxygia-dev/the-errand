## 18. The crows

Grey-light. We wake. We say what is known.

The night was wrong. All of us say it at once, from every cypress, loud: the night was wrong. Lights where there are no lights, white, on poles, down at the water. A roaring that did not stop. Faces all night in the street and at the water and on the path, many, with lights on their heads, calling. The dogs. A strange dog, not ours, heavy, long-eared. The big noise-thing in the sky did not come. Wrong, wrong. It is told, it is told, it is told, until it is put away.

Then quiet. We sit in the tops of the trees with the wet on our backs and look down.

At the gate of the place of the stones, on the wall, two faces.

One is known: the one who puts out bones behind the bar. Gives. She has the smoke. The other is the small face's mother-face, from the house beside the bent one. We have seen her before, always at the window, always looking down at the shining box on her table. Neither. Now she is not at the window. She is on the wall at the gate with a silver thing round her that crackles, and she does not move, and her face is turned up the path where the trees close, and has been turned that way since the dark. We look at it. We learn it. It is a face like the bark of a tree after the rain. It is a face that has been looking at one place for a long time.

The dog goes past. Up. Fast. Into the trees.

We say nothing about the dog. The dog is nuisance.

---

The light comes. The sun gets over the mountain and lands on the tops of the cypresses first, on us, before anything else in the valley. We open our wings in it. We would go down now to the stubble and the bins and the river and the day. But some of us do not go. Some of us stay on the cypresses, a few, with one eye on the place where the trees close.

We do not know why. Nothing is owed.

Then, on the path, coming out of the trees: faces.

Many. Orange and yellow, bright, the colours that shout. Ropes. Boxes on their backs. Slow. And among them, high up, carried on the shoulders of a big face, held — small. Wrapped in silver. A red face, from cold, a small one, looking about.

Looking up.

At us.

And it says the sound. It says it up at the cypresses, at the one with the white feather in his wing, who is on the lowest branch where he always is, too close, where he has been all night. It says it the way it always says it, as if nothing has happened, as if it is any morning at the edge of the school-noise with a crust in its hand:

*Bianchino.*

The one with the white feather puts his head on one side.

---

At the gate the mother-face is up off the wall. The silver thing falls off her. She does not go to the faces coming down; she cannot; she stands at the gate as if the ground has hold of her feet. It is the faces who come to her. The big face takes the small one off his shoulders and puts it down and it stands, and staggers, and the mother-face comes forward then, two steps, and goes down. On her knees. On the wet stones. So her face is the height of the small face.

And they press together. The two faces. Close, closer than faces go, until they are not two shapes but one shape, at the gate, among all the bright faces standing back from it in a ring, quiet.

The mother-face makes a sound. It is not a call. It is not a sound we have. It goes on for a long time.

The dog sits down beside them on the wet stones, square, with his front feet together, and looks at nothing.

---

What is known:

The small face went up the path at the wrong time and did not come down in the dark, and came down in the light, carried. Gives. Small. Looks. Has a sound for one of us. Is kept.

The mother-face: we watch it, in the ring of the bright faces, at the gate, pressed into the small one. We have no verdict. We will watch it. We will see if it comes to the wall.
