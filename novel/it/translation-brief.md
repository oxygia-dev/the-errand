# Translation brief — *The Errand* → *La commissione*

*Written by the author (Claude) for the translators of the Italian edition. This is a self-translation: the author directs it, and the translators should feel free to make the choices an author would make in their own second language. Faithfulness to effect matters more than faithfulness to words.*

## The book in one paragraph

On 29 September, the feast of San Michele, eight-year-old Ida walks up the mountain above the village of Pietrafonda (Abruzzo) with half a loaf of bread, because ninety-year-old Assunta, who has dementia, asked her to take it to her mother. That mother didn't come back in the winter of 1943. Ida delivers the bread, shelters from a storm in a hollow beech, and walks down at dawn. The village searches all night in the wrong places. Every chapter is a different mind with its own perceptual world and its own prose form. **The rule above all: don't make the book explain more in Italian than it does in English.** It's built on restraint and on things not said.

## Voices: what to preserve

**Assunta (1, 11).** Present tense, close third person, time slipping inside sentences. She's sharp and funny. Keep her jokes landing in Italian ("cinque pianeti ed eravamo contenti"). Her dialect lines stay **exactly as written**: *È ita a portà lu pane a mamma*, *Ecco, Mà. Sono arrivata*, *lu pane*. In Italian these will now stand out against the standard Italian, which is correct. Don't add more dialect than the English has.

**The crows (2, 18).** First-person plural, short declaratives, a tiny vocabulary of verdicts. Use these consistently:
- *gives* → **dà**
- *danger* → **pericolo**
- *neither* → **né**
- *nuisance* → **fastidio**
- *faces* → **facce**
- *the small face* → **la faccia piccola**
- *the bent face* → **la faccia curva**
- *the place of the stones* → **il posto delle pietre**
- *the up-trees* → **gli alberi di sopra**
- *the big one* → **la grande**
- *grey-lights* → **luci grigie**
- *It is told* → **È detto**
- *This is known* → **Questo si sa**

**The dog (3, 16).** Present tense, olfactory, no abstractions.
- *biscuit-girl* → **bambina-biscotto**
- *the big one* (the bear) → **la grossa**
- *the yellow one* (biscuit) → **quello giallo**
- *the hook* → **l'amo**
- *the hour of the bones* → **l'ora delle ossa**
- *the strange dog* → **il cane estraneo**

Keep: "Biscuit-girl now walks on biscuit-girl then" → *La bambina-biscotto di adesso cammina sulla bambina-biscotto di allora*.

**Chiara (4, 13).** Conventional, intelligent close third, in the past tense (*passato remoto* / *imperfetto*, literary register). She is a translator. Her riffs on words must be **re-engineered, not literally translated**:
- *should / deve / dovrebbe* already works in Italian.
- *Missing / mancante / vermisst / mi manca* keeps its English and German words as foreign words she thinks about.
- *They are sounding*: keep the English word *sounding* in italics, as the English word she thinks in. Its double sense (probing depth / how it seems) is the point.
- *In piena / sono piena* works natively.

**The tree (5, 15).** Long, slow, impersonal sentences; the most lyrical prose in the book. Present tense. Keep: *Quello che l'albero ha al posto della memoria è la cicatrice.*

**The search logs (6, 14, 17).** Real Italian Carabinieri/VVF report register: terse, nominal style ("Rinvenuto cappello di lana rosso…"). Quoted speech stays in quotes.

**The bees (7).** "Thirty-five." → **Trentacinque.** Plural "we" (noi), dissolving, numerical. Assunta's words to the bees are in italics and very few. Keep them few.

**Rosa (8).** Close third, past tense, a schoolteacher's precision and irritation. Real places: A25, Bussi, Popoli, Sulmona, Piano delle Cinquemiglia, Castel di Sangro, Roccaraso. The road sign: *ATTENZIONE ATTRAVERSAMENTO ORSI*.

**The bear (9).** Slow, appetitive. "She is a hundred and twenty kilos of now." → *È centoventi chili di adesso.* "Hunger is a direction." → *La fame è una direzione.*

**The bat (10).** Clean prose. It uses visual words (*glitters*, *sparkling*, *dull*) knowingly, as if no other words exist. Keep those words visual in Italian (*scintilla*, *luccica*, *opaco*).

**La signorina (12) and the transcript (19).** The device logs are code blocks.
- **Inside code blocks:** keep the machine labels in English (UTTERANCE, EPISODE, class=, conf=, DOOR, RADAR, ASR) exactly. The quoted Italian utterances are already Italian. Translate only free-text English notes inside the logs (e.g. `note: NICOLINO not in profile` → `nota: NICOLINO non presente nel profilo`). Keep alignment roughly.
- **The 23:00 digest** (a blockquote) is fully translated as a pleasant app notification: "Riepilogo giornaliero — Assunta, martedì 29 settembre"; "Consiglio del giorno: …".
- **In chapter 19** the header line `LANG it → en (auto)` becomes `LANG it (originale)   segmenti dialettali: bassa confidenza`. In the Italian edition the transcript is the *original* Italian speech. Translate the dialogue into natural spoken Italian of an eight-year-old Roman girl and a ninety-year-old Abruzzese woman (light regional colour for Assunta, no heavy dialect). Keep the `class=` tags and `[pause]`, `[laughter]`, `[inaudible]` exactly as they are, in English. They're machine annotations.
- Ida's line about pronouncing *Mà* "like a sheep" stays: *Mà. Così. Come una pecora.*

## General rules

- Don't add explanations, footnotes or glosses. Where the English text glosses Italian for English readers (e.g. *"Sono qua, Assù." I'm here.*), **drop the gloss**: Italian readers don't need it.
- Keep every paragraph break, section break (`---`), italic and bold.
- Preserve the markdown structure exactly: headings, blockquotes, code fences.
- Typographic conventions: use Italian *caporali* « » for dialogue in prose chapters (1, 4, 5, 8, 11, 13). In logs and the transcript, keep straight quotes as in the source. Italics for thoughts and foreign words as in the source.
- Numbers and times: 24-hour times as in the source (17:40). Use a decimal comma in prose (35,3 °C) but leave values inside code blocks as they are.
- Register: literary Italian, contemporary, never pompous. When in doubt, choose the plainer word.
