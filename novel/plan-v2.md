# *The Errand* — plan v2

*Supersedes the structural parts of `architecture.md` after the first reader's critique (`process/01`, `process/02`). The rules for each voice in `architecture.md` still apply except where changed here.*

> **She was never lost. She was on an errand and waited out a storm.**
> The job is to stop the structure from being more intelligent than the night.

## Geography (a sketch, for consistency)

- **Pietrafonda**: a fictional village at ~1050 m on the edge of the national park, with about 300 residents in winter. One bar (Bar Centrale), a church (San Michele Arcangelo), a cemetery with cypresses at the top of the village, a stone bridge at the bottom where the road crosses the **Rio Freddo**.
- **The mule track** (*la mulattiera*) leaves from behind the cemetery, climbs through abandoned terraced fields, and enters the beech forest (*la faggeta*) at ~1150 m.
- **The ford** (*lu guado*) at ~1200 m: the Rio Freddo crosses the track on stepping stones, ankle-deep in September. In spate after the storm it's impassable.
- **The great beech**: about 15 minutes above the ford, a little left of the track, roughly 500 years old, with a hollow trunk and an opening at its base "like a door".
- **The Romito**: a shallow cave and ruined hermit's cell at the foot of a limestone cliff at ~1420 m, dedicated to San Michele. It has a stone shelf, a spring dripping from the rock and an iron cross. The procession walks up the mule track every 29 September. A **forestry road** (*strada forestale*) from the pass on the far side reaches within 400 m of it, but it's a long drive round (~50 min).
- **Walking time** for an eight-year-old from village to Romito: about 1h15.
- **The hat**: lost at the ford, found at 22:50 caught in branches below the stone bridge, 1.5 km downstream. The river hypothesis points *down and away*.

## People

- **Assunta** (90). Born 1936, widowed 2009 (Tonino died in the year of the L'Aquila earthquake). A beekeeper for sixty years with three hives left in the garden. She has dementia and sundowns.
- **Maria**, her mother, who didn't come back in December 1943. **Nicolino**, her baby brother, who survived, emigrated to Toronto in 1956 and phones at Christmas.
- **The Englishman**: an escaped POW who slept two nights in the family's stable in October 1943. He told Assunta's nonna you must tell the bees everything. That's one clause, at most two.
- **Rosa** (62). Assunta's daughter, a retired schoolteacher in Pescara. She installed the care device at Easter. Her mother told her the story of the tree once, when Rosa was twelve.
- **Chiara** (38). Ida's mother, a translator from Rome, divorced, who has lived in the village seven months and works remotely. She's a good mother, and new.
- **Ida** (8). Bright, stubborn, a noticer. She named a crow Bianchino and calls the village dog Biscotto. She walked to the Romito in the procession that morning holding Giuseppina's hand.
- **Giuseppina** (74). A neighbour who keeps an eye on Assunta. She took Ida to the procession.
- **Ettore** (the blue gate). Shot at crows in the winter of the deep snow.
- **Brigadiere Lalli**: the kind carabiniere.
- **The dog**: Biscotto, or *Vattenne*. A large mongrel with Maremmano blood, white with grey on his ears, and nobody's.
- **The sow**: a Marsican bear with two cubs of the year.
- **La signorina**: the care device.
