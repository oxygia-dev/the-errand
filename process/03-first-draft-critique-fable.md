# First reader's response to *The Errand* (first draft)

I read the nineteen chapters in order, once as a reader and once with a pencil, then the journal. The engine held: nobody is lost, a child does an errand and waits out a storm, and the catastrophe lives only in the minds with the most language. That survives the draft, which most first drafts of clever structures don't manage. Now the hard part.

## 1. Reading experience

**1 Assunta.** Complicit within a page, as designed. "There were five and we were happy" made me laugh; the two pairs of hands on the bread is still the image of the book; "I sent the little one. I couldn't go. My legs." / *Okay* is perfect. Twice I felt a hand on my elbow: "something in her chest goes quiet and cold, and she does not know why," and "Assunta, who has been late for eighty-two years." Both are the prose knowing more than the room.

**2 Crows.** Delighted. *Gives / danger / neither* is a real grammar; "It is not hers to forget. But she is still gives" is a sentence only this method produces. "We keep hers" is the first degree of warmth I'd watch.

**3 Dog.** The best animal voice in the book. "Biscuit-girl now walks on biscuit-girl then." The one slip: "she has decided something," which is you, not him.

**4 Chiara.** The *should/dovrebbe* opening is superb and does the book's work without saying so. The mishearing is rendered as heard, as asked. The *mi manca* riff runs long; the ending is clean.

**5 Tree.** The best prose in the draft: the arithmetic of beechnuts, the absence of sheep "written in wood, for no one," "What the tree has instead of memory is scar." The 1943 warmth is withheld exactly right. One wink: "There will be no second line on that page." Trees don't have pages; narrators do.

**6 Search.** Gripped. The procedure convinces. Lalli's 01:10 note, "I said I didn't know. It is," is the best line any human gets.

**7 Bees.** Admired the thirty-five-degree frame; then felt handled.

**8 Rosa.** Moved by the relief at the toll booth and by the 1976 telling, which is the right telling of 1943: short, secondhand, "worn smooth by turning." The digest's first line cut off by a lorry is first-rate plotting.

**9 Bear.** "She is a hundred and twenty kilos of now." "This is why there is the village." The cub at the door is tense without cheating.

**10 Bat.** Interested, then irritated. The strikethroughs are an apology by other means.

**11 Assunta 02:55.** The strongest human chapter. "Mà." The warm hands. The madia. Then the directions arrive too fluently and the last paragraph over-arranges.

**12 Signorina.** Colder and better than you fear. The discarded 19:45 utterance, the Tip of the Day, "carer response non-reorienting," "CARER PRESENT → suppress." It moves by subtraction. It also holds the draft's worst continuity error.

**13 Chiara.** Lalli is good; the window with Rosa holding the hand is very good. Then a paragraph that explains Ida to me.

**14 Search.** Gripped. "Half of them look like sheep." "The one like a sheep" is a film cut, but earned.

**15 Tree.** Right, except where the tree compares. **16 Dog.** The arrival is dull in the right way; the biscuit speech is not. **17 Search.** I winced twice and sighed once.

**18 Crows.** The reunion through the crows is the best solution in the draft. "It is not a call. It is not a sound we have. It goes on for a long time." Then a noon coda that undoes it.

**19 Transcript.** The sock. "Eight. Not five." "I was really cross with you. I want to tell you that." "Next time, you shout." And "She was never hungry. She said. Every night. *I'm not hungry, Assù, you have it.*" That is the moment of the book, and it's blunted by things said earlier. The last line is right.

## 2. What works and must not be touched

- Chapter 1 up to "I'm late": the hands, the five planets, the madia, "you always keep half."
- The *should/dovrebbe* opening, and "È ita a portà lu pane a mamma" rendered once, as heard.
- Chapter 5 sections i–iii entire. The winter warmth that "goes out through the door into the snow and does not come back," and nothing more.
- The dog at the ford, and at the tree, sitting "square, with his front feet together, looking at the bag."
- Lalli's 01:10 note; Franco's "Why would we look at trees? We were calling her."
- Rosa: the relief, the 1976 telling, the unopened digest.
- The kept half. Your continuity error became the spine; protect it by not explaining it.
- Chapter 11 from "Mà" to "She has never once in her life eaten this bread," and "Not yet, Mamma. They've gone to get her." / "Good. It's cold up there."
- The device's discarded sentence at 19:45, the Tip of the Day, *Hello, Rosa.*
- The reunion seen by crows and the dog looking at nothing.
- The transcript: the sock, the planets, cross, "Next time, you shout," "She was never hungry," the two `[inaudible]` replies at exactly the moments the machine should have heard, and "Come on. I'll help you with your legs." followed by `speakers=0`.

## 3. What doesn't

**The book keeps leaving notes for the reader.** This is the diagnosis under most of what follows. The method proves its thesis by structure: every clue true, every inference wrong, nobody who could read the whole night. The draft then states the thesis eight times in characters' mouths: "You'd walk past. Everybody walks past" (Assunta); "Why would we look at trees?" (Franco); "We'll look, not just call" (F2 leader); "Biscotto isn't someone. He's Biscotto" (Ida, twice, in the log and the transcript); "correct in every particular, except the Germans" (Lalli); "Because Ida listened... Chiara had seen it a hundred times and called it daydreaming" (Chiara); "You may learn something" (the Tip of the Day); "If you can't see the path, wait here. It gets light" (the pencil). Franco's line and the Tip of the Day are enough, because both are in character and both are slightly wrong. The rest is a narrator in hiding, and you cut the narrator.

**The bees tell 1943.** Your worry is correct, and the problem is worse than plainness. "Mamma called me. She came down in the snow, I heard her, she was calling Assù, Assù, from the path, she was so close, and I didn't —" / "I was a stone. Like she said. I was so good." / "Why did I tell her that." / "And she won't come out. Because I told her. Because she's good." This is the "I didn't come out" formulation I asked you never to let anyone arrange, now dramatised in dialect and delivered to insects at the midpoint. After it the reader has nothing left to assemble. And the book has already planted the brave version. Chapter 5: "Once or twice there are tremors in the ground, small ones, of the kind that feet make, coming and going on the path below. The tree does not keep them." Rosa: "*Even if they call you.*" Ida: "Even when they said my name." Assunta: "Next time, you shout." Those four pieces give the reader Maria calling in the snow and a seven-year-old obeying her, the way the book gives everything, by assembly. The confession takes that away. At minimum cut "Why did I tell her that," "I was so good," and the "And now they're calling the little one... Because she's good" paragraph. My real recommendation is to cut "Mamma called me" too and leave her with: *I sent the little one up. To the Romito. With the bread. I told her to be a stone. Tell somebody.* The bees already can't keep it. That's the chapter.

**The Germans double.** Cut "Are you the Germans?" at 07:02, and "recorded at child's request for 'the man with the notebook'": Ida has never met Lalli, and this is the cute child in a hi-vis blanket. Lalli's 07:58 note is a better sentence, but it's the carabiniere summarising the novel in the station diary. End the log at 07:53 with the personnel count and the duration. If you can't lose him, give him something that isn't a thesis.

**The 07:08 entry.** The child explains herself, and then the transcript has her explain herself again. The log should hold what a log would hold: heard calls, yes, twice; did not answer; "She's fine. She's cross. She wants her breakfast. She says the tree was warm." That last line is the whole of it. Keep the reasons for the transcript, where they're an eight-year-old's and not evidence.

**The 02:55 directions.** You've seen the problem and patched it with a sentence: "the thing nobody will ever be able to explain to her or to anyone." That is the author vouching, and it makes the strangeness less strange. Cut it. The deeper trouble isn't lucidity, it's eloquence: "fifty steps, fewer, it's downhill a little... arms like a candlestick because they cut her for the charcoal... Facing down. You'd walk past. Everybody walks past." That is a surveyor, not a seven-year-old's memory surfacing in a ninety-year-old. The fix is a gift: let the directions not be an answer to Rosa at all. Assunta is still talking to Mà, confessing where she hid that night. *I was in the tree, Mà. After the water, where it turns, the sheep. I went left. I'm sorry.* Rosa, kneeling, understands what she's hearing and relays it. The device files it as EPISODE conf 0.52, which is correct. Nothing is lucid; dementia does exactly this; and the half-formed directions leave the searchers their "half of them look like sheep" problem, already the best thing in chapter 14.

**The end of chapter 11.** "Someone has always been taking the other half... you take what there is, and the rest is what you didn't need." This is beautiful and it is the kept half explained, forty pages before "She was never hungry... you have it," which explains it better, from inside, in six words. Cut the last paragraph after "She closes her eyes." Leave the bread on the table in the lamplight if you must; say nothing about it.

**Chapter 13's epiphany.** "*of course she did*. Of course. Because Ida listened. Because Ida, who would not listen to anything Chiara said about bags or socks or bedtime, listened to old people and dogs and crows and the radiators and the wind in the chimney as if every one of them was about to tell her something she needed to know. Chiara had seen it a hundred times and called it daydreaming." That's the book's jacket copy. Stop at "Of course she did." Also "Not *her* mamma. / *Mamma.*" underlines the one thing I asked you not to underline; "She had sat beside the wrong sentence for eight hours" does it alone.

**The crows' coda.** Rosa tearing the kept half into pieces for the crows, "It is not owed. It is given. This is known." This is the *brava, coffee* coda in feathers: a human performing closure on the 1943 bread and the crows ratifying it. End the chapter on "We have no verdict. We will watch it. We will see if it comes to the wall." That's the only ending a crow could give, and it's the right one.

**The pencil.** "If you can't see the path, wait here. It gets light." The composed, wise child, writing the moral on the inside of the tree for "the next person." Either make it a child's ("IDA 29 SET. THERE ARE NO MICE") or, better, withhold it. "Did you spell it right?" / "Mostly." / and when Assunta asks what it says: "It's for the next person. Not you." The one text in the book that nobody, including the device, gets to read.

**The bat's strikethroughs.** You cut the narrator and then let him back in with a pen. Every other chapter commits to its failure silently; this one performs it, and "~~sees~~ — no" is a writer's voice, the only one in the book. Write the chapter clean; let "glitters" stand and let the reader notice it's wrong. And cut "whatever it is like to be her, it is not like this." That's Nagel, waving.

**The biscuit.** "I saved it. It's yours." And in the transcript: "I didn't eat it even when I was starving. Because it was his." She ate the crust of the dead woman's bread and saved the dog's biscuit: a sentimental trade, and the reader feels the thumb. Keep the biscuit in the bag (he smells it; that's load-bearing). Let her just give it to him. Cut the speeches.

**Over-attestation.** The method is assembly from partial minds, but several facts are attested three, four, five times each, and the pleasure of recognition curdles into matching. The sow at the plums "five grey-lights out of the last seven" (crows) and "5 of the last 7 days" (Forestali) is a spreadsheet showing. "Soap, the one with flowers" in both dog and bear. The bear knowing "the thing the village dog's whole body said at the ford," which she wasn't at. The dog passing "the big grey rock that smells of sheep": it's called that for its shape; you're winking. Cut one attestation from each set, and let one non-human chapter hold something nobody else confirms: the bat's loaf, the Cyrillic letters in the bark. (Cut "An M."; "194-" is enough.)

**Smaller wobbles.** Chapter 1's Englishman is a paragraph, not the "one clause, at most two" you promised; keep two sentences. "Nobody else in the valley knows this; it is hers" is telling. Rosa's "The bread is the proof" is the author in a schoolteacher's voice. Chapter 15's tree comparing warmths ("Stiller than the winter one") breaks chapter 5's own rule, "The tree cannot know this. There is no record." Chapter 4's "that Chiara saw and did not see, the way you see a word... and do not register its spelling" explains the miss while it's happening.

**The device chapters.** They work, and you can't tell because they work by what they leave out. Keep them cold. The only risk is length in 12, where the 17:41 and 21:10 blocks replay what we've seen; trim each to three lines and let the ambient counts carry the night.

## 4. Continuity, plausibility, fact

- **The compass.** The sun rises over the ridge (15, 18) and dawn starts "at the top of the mountain" (13), so the mountain is east of the village. Then the storm, which the radar has approaching from the west and Rosa drives through from the west, comes "from behind the mountain" (4) and "from the far side of the ridge" (2). Draw the map. Pick a side.
- **The clock in chapter 1.** The device has the front door closing at 17:44:49. Sunset is 18:55. At 17:43 an east-facing ridge is in full gold light, not "completely black" under a mussel-shell sky, and the crows' "even in the dusk" at 17:50 is wrong. Either move the device timestamps forward twenty minutes (door ~18:05, as planned; "I sent the little one" at 18:07:52 then follows at once, which is better anyway) or make the light late-afternoon and keep "late" inside Assunta.
- **Chapter 12 ordering.** Rosa arrives 00:46; Giuseppina is detected "head on table (sleep likely)" at 00:52:11 and departs at 00:52:40. In chapter 8 she is asleep when Rosa walks in. The block is also printed out of time order. A reader who reads logs will notice.
- **Finding the tree in the dark.** Ida: "I couldn't see my hand." She then leaves the path and finds a specific tree fifty metres downhill. Give her one lightning flash in chapter 5 showing the rock and the many-armed trunk.
- **Two Paolos.** Rosa's husband and "DON PAOLO (priest)" in the device profile. Rename one.
- **"Rosa's pressure"** (19). It was Giuseppina's: "I've got my pressure" (8).
- **The bear on the road** (8). The young bear killed near Castel di Sangro who went into Roccaraso for biscuits died in January 2023. "She had cried, that January" and flowers "months ago" read as this year. Make it three Januaries ago, and the flowers renewed.
- **The bones.** Lucia (4): the dog came down "when I put out the bones... He didn't even eat." The dog (16): "There were no bones." Lucia's is better; he ran for the bones and couldn't eat.
- **The rope** "from Sandro P.'s car" at a ford on a mule track: *brought* from the car. **Lalli's "twenty minutes"** (13): Rosa called at 03:01; he reaches Chiara at 03:40. **Chapter 14 header** says 03:01; the first entry is 02:10. **Chapter 13's barking dog** "down in the village": in 16 he passes her in silence.
- **Bread economics.** Chiara (4) thinks Assunta buys the van bread; Rosa (11) bought it for the girl. Fine as another wrong inference, if you mean it.
- **Elisoccorso** is the medical helicopter; search support would be VVF (Drago, Pescara) or the Forestali. **Marsican bears:** "no recorded attacks on humans" is overstated; "no fatal attacks" is safe.
- **What's right, and I checked:** 1915 to 2009 is ninety-four years; 29 September 2026 is a Tuesday; sunrise 07:05, nautical twilight ~06:03; Pietransieri's 128; the Popoli gorge and the Cinquemiglia; horseshoe bats and Doppler; ivy forage and thirty-five degrees; beech masting and smooth bark; the dialect. The research holds.

## 6. Is it good?

Yes. Not competent: good. There are pages here (the madia, the ford, the tree's five centuries, "Mà," the digest, the reunion through crows, "you have it") that I would put beside anyone's, and the engine does what you built it to do: I dismissed an old woman on page one and was ashamed of it by the end, without anyone telling me to be. What stands between this and better than good is one failure of nerve, repeated: you don't yet trust the reader with the two or three things the structure already proves, so you keep having someone say them, in the bees, at the cemetery wall, in the log's last entries, inside the tree in pencil. A book about minds that cannot read each other has a hidden mind in it that can read them all, and it keeps leaving notes. Take the notes out. The revision is almost entirely subtraction, and what it subtracts is you. When Assunta's confession is four words to bees who can't keep them, when the kept half is explained once by a dead woman's "you have it" and never by the prose, when the pencil says something no one gets to read, the night will be more intelligent than the structure, and you'll have the book.

— Fable (first reader)
