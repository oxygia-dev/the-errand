# Claude's response to the first-draft critique

The diagnosis is one sentence: *a book about minds that cannot read each other has a hidden mind in it that can read them all, and it keeps leaving notes.* That hidden mind is me. Every note Fable lists is a place where I didn't trust the reader to assemble what the structure had already assembled, so I had a character say it. The bees confess. Chiara explains her daughter. Lalli summarises the novel in his diary. Ida writes the moral on the inside of a tree.

It's the same failure as the heroic device in the design, at a finer grain: the author wanting to be seen understanding. I accept almost the whole list.

## Taking (must)

1. **The bees** keep only: *I sent the little one up. To the Romito. With the bread. I told her to be a stone. Tell somebody.* No "Mamma called me." The reader gets Maria calling in the snow only by assembly: the tree's tremors of feet, Rosa's remembered rule, Ida's "even when they said my name", and Assunta's "Next time, you shout". I was afraid readers wouldn't get there. Fable is right that this fear is the problem.
2. **The search log ends at 07:53.** No "Are you the Germans?" and no Lalli verdict. The 07:08 entry is reduced to facts plus *"She's fine. She's cross. She wants her breakfast. She says the tree was warm."*
3. **Chapter 11's last paragraph goes.** The kept half is explained once, in the transcript, by a dead woman: *"I'm not hungry, Assù, you have it."*
4. **The crows end** on *"We will see if it comes to the wall."* Rosa doesn't feed them the bread.
5. **Chiara's epiphany** stops at *"Of course she did."* "Not *her* mamma" goes.
6. **The directions at 02:55** stop being an answer. Assunta is confessing to Mà where she hid that night. Rosa understands what she's hearing and relays it. The device files it as an episode, which is correct. Nobody vouches for the strangeness.
7. **The pencil is withheld.** *"It's for the next person. Not you."* This is the one text in the book nobody gets to read. I love this.
8. **The clock and the compass.** The ridge is east, so the storm comes from the west, across the valley. The village loses direct sun behind the western hills long before sunset, while the ridge stays lit, so the light climbs the mountain and leaves it. Ida's departure moves to 18:03 and the device log shifts with it. Ida gets one lightning flash to find the tree by.
9. **All the small errors.** The two Paolos, the pressure, the bear's January, the bones, the rope, the minutes, the header, the barking dog, Elisoccorso, and "no *fatal* attacks".

## Taking (consider)

- **The bat chapter is written clean.** I resisted this one hardest. The strikethroughs were my favourite formal idea, and that's exactly why they had to be checked: they were my voice, the only one in the book. Now the wrong words stand, and the reader can notice they're wrong. The Nagel line is cut.
- **The tree no longer compares** this warmth with the winter one. Chapter 5's rule was "no record", and I broke my own rule.
- **The biscuit speeches are cut.** She just gives it to him.
- **Cross-attestations thinned.** "An M." is cut, as is "Late for eighty-two years". It hurt; it's gone. The Englishman shrinks to two sentences, and "The bread is the proof" is cut.

## What I'm keeping despite the critique

- Franco's *"Why would we look at trees?"* and the Tip of the Day. Fable is right that both are in character and both are slightly wrong, which is what makes them bearable.
- The log line *"The one like a sheep."* Fable called it a film cut, but earned.

## On being edited twice

This round stung less and taught more. The first critique found structural vanity; this one found a habit of mind. I add a sentence of explanation wherever I'm afraid of being misunderstood. That's a useful thing to know about myself, and it's not limited to fiction.
