# Italian edit report — *La commissione*

Read: the brief, the assembled edition (`book/la-commissione.md`), and the English originals wherever a quotation or a doubtful rendering needed checking. All edits were made in `novel/it/chapters/*.md`, `novel/it/note-dal-campo.md` and `novel/it/postfazione.md`; nothing was committed and the generated book file was not touched.

## What I changed

**Cross-file quotations (made identical to the chapters' actual wording)**
- *Five planets.* The ch. 12 log line at 17:39:40 now quotes ch. 1's first wording exactly: `"ai tempi miei c'erano cinque pianeti e bastavano"` (the English log also quotes the "that was plenty" line, not the "we were happy" line). The afterword's «ed eravamo contenti» correctly quotes the second line and was left alone.
- *Assunta's mother's line.* Kept ch. 19's spoken version, «Non tengo fame, Assù, mangialo tu» (light southern colour in *tengo*, and *mangialo* renders "you have it" more closely than *mangia tu*). The afterword now quotes the whole ch. 19 passage verbatim: *«Lei fame non ne aveva mai. Diceva. Ogni sera. Non tengo fame, Assù, mangialo tu.»* The "sei parole" claim remains true (non / tengo / fame / Assù / mangialo / tu), so it stays.
- *Ida's inscription.* Afterword now matches ch. 19: «È per chi ci viene dopo. Non per te.»
- *Half-loaf rule.* Afterword now quotes ch. 1 as written: *perché metà si tiene sempre. Non si manda mai tutto. Gliel'ha insegnato Mamma, prima ancora dell'Ave Maria.*
- *"Still like a stone."* Afterword now uses ch. 1's phrases (*Se senti parlare tedesco stai ferma. Stai ferma come un sasso. Anche se qualcuno ti chiama. Anche se è uno che conosci.*), which also keeps it consistent with ch. 14's «anche se è uno che conosci».
- *Assunta's directions to the tree.* Ch. 14's quoted fragment now matches ch. 11 and the ch. 12 log: «quello grosso, quello tagliato» (was «quello che hanno tagliato»).
- *Bees' question.* Ch. 19 «Sei stata dalle api?» → «Sei andata dalle api?», the same question as ch. 1 (the device tags it as perseveration, so it must be the same words).
- *Field notes:* the bat's moth is now «un tremolio nel tono» (ch. 10's actual words); the crows' «del colore che le facce non vedono»; the tree's «non sa nemmeno questo» and «pieni di braccia»; the bees «non conservino»; the dog handler's reading «la traccia finisce all'acqua»; and "tutto il paese odorava di paura" is no longer in quotation marks, since ch. 16 says *ne odorava*.
- Afterword "quattro righe" → "tre righe": the bees' confession is three italic lines in both the English and the Italian chapter (the English afterword's "four" is a miscount; see open questions).

**Voice and consistency**
- *Crows' "we":* ch. 2 used masculine agreement (nessuno di noi, fatti, qualcuno, gli ultimi, l'uno… dell'altro) while ch. 18 used feminine (qualcuna, poche). Now feminine throughout, as *le cornacchie* requires. Bianchino stays «quello con la penna bianca» (he is named and male in the English); ch. 2 «piuma» unified to «penna» as in ch. 18. Also «Non sta a lei dimenticare» → «Non è da lei, dimenticare» ("It is not hers to forget").
- *Dog/crows:* ch. 18 «gli ossi» → «le ossa» (ch. 3, 4, 16 all say *ossa*); the foil blanket «scricchiola» → «crepita» (ch. 13 and 16 both have *crepita*; the English is *crackles* in all three).
- *The device:* ch. 11 «dice la macchina» → «dice la macchinetta», the word used everywhere else in prose. Spoken replies are now verified identical across ch. 1, 11 and 12 («Sono qui, Assunta. Come posso aiutarti?», «Mi dispiace, non ho capito. Vuoi che chiami Rosa?», «Okay», «Ciao, Rosa»).
- *Logs:* ch. 6 «capo squadra» → «caposquadra» (ch. 14, 17).
- Verified already consistent, no change needed: *cappello* for Ida's hat in every chapter (ch. 13's *berretto* is the old men's caps); *tepore* in ch. 5 and 15; *guado*, *mulattiera*, *la pecora / sasso della pecora*, *capitozzato / capitozza*, sector letters V/R/M/F, «Se l'è presa la pioggia» (ch. 14 and 16); Assunta's dialect lines; the digest; `LANG it (originale)` header; `[pause]`/`[laughter]` tags; all device labels in English.

**Typography**
- Three different dialogue-tag conventions were in use. Ch. 11 («Mà», dice) and ch. 13 («Signora,» disse) now follow the majority convention of ch. 1, 4, 5, 8: «Sono in ritardo» dice — no comma. No straight quotes remain in prose chapters; logs and transcript keep straight quotes as the brief asks.

## What I deliberately did not change
- Ch. 4, the italic «*È andata a portare il pane alla mamma.*» after Assunta's dialect line. The brief says to drop glosses, but here the English line is Chiara's own act of translating (set up by "doveva ancora farsi passare due volte per la testa" and paid off in ch. 13, "la frase tradotta male"). I kept it as characterisation. Author's call.
- Ch. 4 «Bar Central» in the second instance: the English does the same (the sign with the dead letter), so it is intentional.
- Ch. 8 «*Vuoi che guido io*» (indicative in spoken Italian) and Ida's Roman colloquialisms (*cozzetto*, *c'avevo*, *'sta roba*, *casomai*): correct register, left alone.
- Ch. 12 POWER lines out of chronological order: same in the English source.

## Open questions for the author
1. The English afterword says the bees' confession became "four lines"; the chapter has three. I changed the Italian to "tre righe". If you want the English fixed too, it is the same sentence.
2. The ch. 4 gloss question above.
3. Ch. 19: Ida says the thing at the door smelled «tipo un armadio vecchio» and ch. 3 gives the old woman "un odore come il dentro di un armadio". In the English both are wardrobe smells too, so this is presumably an intended rhyme (bear/old woman) rather than an error; flagging only because a sharp reader will notice.

## Assessment
This is a genuinely good Italian edition, not a translated one. The strongest chapters are the tree (5, 15), the bear, the dog and Assunta (1, 11): the Italian has its own rhythm, the restraint survives, and nothing explains more than the English does. Chiara's word-riffs (*dovrebbe*, *vermisst/mi manca*, *sounding*, *in piena/sono piena*) are properly re-engineered rather than translated. The logs read like real Italian verbali. Ida's speech in ch. 19 is convincingly an eight-year-old Roman, and Assunta's regional colour is light and exact. The five translators' seams were almost entirely at the level of punctuation and gendered agreement, which is the best kind of seam to have; after this pass I do not hear a change of voice between blocks. Remaining risk is the one the author already names: the dialect lines want an alto Sangro reader.
