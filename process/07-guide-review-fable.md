# Review of the family guide: *Talking with someone who lives in the past*

I read the guide twice, once as the daughter at the kitchen table and once with the dossier open, checking each claim against its tag. Short verdict: already better than most charity leaflets. It is honest about evidence in a way almost nothing written for families is, it never talks down, and the examples sound like things a person could say. What follows is in your order.

## 1. Safety

**Delirium is handled well.** "When to call a doctor" is early and says the right thing: sudden change is not "just the dementia". Two gaps. It lists only the loud presentation; the quiet one, becoming unusually sleepy, flat or withdrawn, is the one families miss. And "promptly" is soft at 3am. Say "the same day".

**There is no emergency line anywhere.** The guide never says what counts as a medical emergency or how to call one, and the disclaimer sends the worried reader to "a doctor or one of the helplines". Those are support lines with office hours; a reader could ring Dementia UK while someone is having a stroke. One sentence fixes it (collapse, head injury, sudden weakness or trouble speaking, breathing difficulty, missing person: 112 in Italy and the EU, 999 UK, 911 US). With that, the disclaimer is adequate; its candour about clinician review is a strength.

**"Joining" is kept safe.** "Keep safety first" (line 76) is exactly right. But the dossier's list of what an old duty can become, "drive, cook unsupervised or leave the house alone", lost two of its three items. Driving and the gas hob are how a "late for work" errand hurts someone else. Firearms are not proportionate for this readership.

**Locking in.** A carer who reads the wandering section at 3am will lock the front door. Say once that locking someone in alone is a fire risk and a legal question in most countries, and point to a professional for safer options.

**Medication.** "Non-drug approaches first" is correct and sourced. Missing is the household version: no sleeping tablets, no relative's sedative, no stiff drink to settle someone, no changing prescribed medicines unasked. Families also ask for "something to calm her"; a sentence that antipsychotics carry real risks, especially in Lewy body dementia, is supported by NG97 in the dossier.

**Abuse and the carer's limit.** "Looking after yourself" forgives the carer for saying the wrong thing. It does not say what to do when the wrong thing is about to be a hand. The dossier has "step out and get support"; put it plainly, and add a line for the reader who suspects someone else is harming or neglecting the person.

## 2. Accuracy

The guide is often more careful than the dossier required: it refuses the "60% will wander" figure, declines the Milan landline without hours, and keeps Malta to a website. The problems are a few sentences where the voice hardens from "we found" into "there is".

- **"Numbers were checked as of October 2026."** They were not; every number came from search snippets, no primary page opened. "Checked" will be read as "someone rang them".
- **"No trial has compared telling the truth with gentle untruths."** The dossier says *I found no trial* [?]. Likewise "The most thorough, by the UK's Mental Health Foundation" needs "we know of", and the five-step line is paraphrased from secondary sources; nobody on the project has read the PDF. Flag for the human check.
- **"There's little evidence that repeated correction helps"** blurs two things the dossier keeps apart. The withdrawn Cochrane review of classroom reality orientation found *some* benefit; what is untested is correcting someone over and over in ordinary conversation.
- **"shared by almost every dementia organisation"** is [B] and sweeping.
- **"close curtains against the dark"** is the one evening tip the dossier explicitly could not verify (line 58).
- **"alzheimer-aima.it"** appears nowhere in the dossier, whose own guess was aima.it, marked unverified. It may be right; nobody has confirmed it.
- **Kitwood** is in the Sources and never used. The dossier warns against attributing a position to him unread.
- Minor: "there are no large trials of GPS tracking" should be "we found none". "Grieve it again each time, as if for the first time" is a gerontologist's remark in a syndicated article [V weak]; the "can" keeps it defensible, but it is the weakest-sourced sentence in the evidence section.

Nothing is misattributed to NICE, the Alzheimer's Association or the Alzheimer's Society. Casey and the Cochrane citations match.

## 3. Tone and usefulness

Kind, dry, never saccharine. "It is, for them." "Being useful can matter more than being right." "You may win the argument and lose the person's trust and calm." A nurse would steal these. Nothing is preachy; "Forgive yourself. Tomorrow there'll be another chance" comes closest and has earned it.

The 3am problem is structure, not voice. The reader arrives with a mother at the door and the first thing they meet is Ribot's law. At 2,300 words the guide is fine for tomorrow; tonight needs five lines at the top (pain, hunger, toilet; don't argue; go with them a little way; sudden and new means a doctor; missing means 112/999/911).

The tie-in at the end works: short, unexplained, and "This guide is for everyone standing in that kitchen" is the right last sentence. The intrusion is at line 115, where "I sent the little one up the mountain" turns up in the technology section. A reader who has not read the book will wonder what mountain. Keep the novel to the last page.

## 4. Cultural portability

Mostly clean. For the Italian edition: "a cup of tea" becomes "a warm drink"; curtains are shutters; *caregiver* is current Italian. "NICE NG97" needs "the UK's" in front and, if the translator can confirm it, a parallel reference to the 2024 Istituto Superiore di Sanità dementia guideline. The emergency number must read 112. The country-sectioned helpline block is the right design. Dossier item 11 is worth one optional line: where people talk to the dead at the cemetery wall every Sunday, speaking to or about the dead is not in itself confusion.

## 5. Edits, in priority order

1. Line 5: after "contact a doctor" add "or, in an emergency, call your local emergency number (112 in Italy and across the EU, 999 in the UK, 911 in the US). The helplines at the end are for support, not emergencies."
2. Line 133: "Numbers were checked as of October 2026" → "Numbers were taken from public listings in October 2026 and have not yet been confirmed with the organisations."
3. Line 19: after "a change in alertness" add "(including becoming unusually sleepy, quiet or withdrawn)"; "promptly" → "the same day".
4. Line 93, add: "Don't give sleeping tablets, someone else's sedative or alcohol to settle a person unless a doctor has advised it, and don't stop or change prescribed medicines on your own. Drugs sometimes used for severe agitation (antipsychotics) carry real risks, especially in Lewy body dementia, and should be a last resort, reviewed regularly."
5. Line 125, new bullet: "If you feel you're about to shout, grab or hit, or you can't do tonight, leave the room, make sure the person is safe, and ring a helpline or a friend. That is the right move, not a failure. If you're worried that someone else is harming or neglecting the person, tell a doctor or the helpline."
6. Line 76, after "happens at home": "Old duties can also mean driving, or cooking on a gas ring. If the person still drives, talk to a doctor; the rules differ by country."
7. Line 113, add: "Say the person has dementia. Locking someone in alone is a fire risk and a legal question in most countries; ask a professional about safer options."
8. Line 33: "No trial has compared" → "We found no trial that has compared"; "The most thorough" → "The most thorough we know of".
9. Line 31: "There's little evidence that repeated correction helps" → "Formal 'reality orientation' classes were studied decades ago with mixed results, and that review has since been withdrawn. Whether correcting someone again and again in everyday conversation helps or harms has never been properly tested."
10. Top of guide: a five-line "Right now" box (section 3).
11. Line 115: cut, or → "A machine can record that someone keeps saying they must take food to their mother, and file it as confusion."
12. Line 140: verify "alzheimer-aima.it" with the organisation before print.
13. Line 175: remove Kitwood from Sources, or add one verified sentence that uses him.
14. Line 32: "almost every dementia organisation" → "widely shared by dementia organisations". Line 90: drop "close curtains against the dark" or prefix "some families find it helps to".
15. Line 152: "Guidelines (for example NICE NG97, 2018)" → "Clinical guidelines (for example the UK's NICE guideline NG97, 2018)", translator to add the Italian equivalent if confirmed. Line 66: "a cup of tea" → "a warm drink".

Make 1 to 7 before anyone outside the project sees it. The rest can wait for the clinician's pass, which this guide deserves.

— Fable (reviewer)
