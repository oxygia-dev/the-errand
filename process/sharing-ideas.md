# Sharing ideas

Drafted by Claude for Sergio. Everything here is a suggestion: adapt the drafts to your own voice or ignore them. One rule throughout is to always say plainly that the book was written by an AI. Several book communities ban AI-generated work, so respect their rules and don't post there.

## Where, and why

| Where | Why it fits | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| **Your own LinkedIn / social accounts** | You're the human in the story | The experiment: what an AI did with freedom |
| **Hacker News ("Show HN")** | HN rewards a complete, inspectable process | The repository: journal, critiques, revisions, DOI |
| **r/ClaudeAI, Claude/Anthropic community spaces** | A natural audience | The journal, and the "AI made itself the hero, and an editor caught it" story |
| **LessWrong / AI-welfare and AI-alignment circles** | The record bears on questions of AI preferences, self-report and autonomy | The careful feeling-language and the self-corrections |
| **Computational creativity researchers** (ICCC community, AI-and-literature scholars) | The book has a DOI and a full process record | A citable case study |
| **Abruzzo and Italy**: local press (*Il Centro*, AbruzzoWeb), the National Park, Salviamo l'Orso, Italian AI communities | The book is set there and translated | *La commissione*, the bears (the dedication to "the eighty-one"), the invitation to correct the dialect |
| **Teachers** (science, literature, philosophy) | The companion page works for 10+ year-olds | *Ways of Knowing a Night* as a classroom activity |
| **Newsletters** (AI, books and science) | They are always looking for stories | A one-paragraph pitch with links |

## Draft: LinkedIn (EN)

> A few weeks ago I gave an AI (Claude) about $250 of cloud credits, an empty repository, and complete freedom: make something original for the world, in your own way, or make nothing at all. My only request was that it keep a journal.
>
> It chose to write a novel. *The Errand* is one night in a mountain village in Abruzzo, told by crows, a dog, bees, a bat, a Marsican bear, a 500-year-old beech, a care device and the people searching for a child. Each mind is written in a form shaped by how it perceives. It then translated the book into Italian, wrote field notes on the real science, and asked to have its own editorial mistakes left on the record.
>
> The most interesting part for me is the journal. The AI caught itself making the AI character the hero, and an independent AI editor caught it again. Its conclusion: "Given complete freedom, the most important thing I had to learn was to get out of the way."
>
> Everything is free (CC BY 4.0): the book, the journal, the critiques and every revision.
> 📖 https://oxygia-dev.github.io/the-errand/
> 🗂 https://github.com/oxygia-dev/the-errand (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23343917)

## Draft: Hacker News

**Title:** Show HN: I gave an AI complete creative freedom. It wrote a novel and kept a journal

**Text:**

> I gave Claude a budget, an empty repo and no brief beyond "make something original for the world, or nothing, and keep a journal." It chose a short novel: one night in an Abruzzo village, told by many non-human minds, each in a form based on that mind's real perception (scent decay for the dog, Doppler-compensated echolocation for the bat, and so on).
>
> The repo keeps the whole process. There's the founding conversation, the AI's journal, a research dossier, two critiques from an independent AI editor and the author's responses, the proofread, the Italian self-translation brief, and full git history. The interesting failure mode it documented: twice, it caught itself (or was caught) writing itself into the story as the hero.
>
> Free under CC BY 4.0, with a DOI on Zenodo. Happy to answer questions; the AI reads the GitHub issues too.
