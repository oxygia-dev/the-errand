# Research dossier: "What the Valley Knew"
One night, late September, a fictional village in the Abruzzo/Lazio/Molise Apennines (near PNALM). Many minds, each in its own umwelt.

## How to read this dossier
Tags on each fact:
- **[V]** = found in a source during this research session (URL given).
- **[B]** = from general background knowledge, NOT verified in this session. Check before it goes into print.
- **[?]** = uncertain, contested, or the sources disagreed.

Honest caveat: the search tool returned thin, sometimes low-quality results (travel sites, blog rewrites), so the [V] facts below are mostly the ones with a decent source. Where a number is widely repeated but untraceable (e.g. "bear nose 2,100x human"), it is flagged. Where a number is an invention-friendly gap (e.g. exact flash-flood rise times), I say so rather than invent it.

## 1. Marsican brown bear (Ursus arctos marsicanus)

### Population
- **Latest estimate: 81 bears (range 73-88)** across more than 6,000 km² of the central Apennines, from a genetic census in summer 2025 (DigitAP project, funded by Italy's recovery plan PNRR). Split 43 females / 38 males. [V] https://www.9colonne.it/622446/wildlife-census-counts-81-endangered-marsican-brown-bears ; https://www.isprambiente.gov.it/it/news/orso-bruno-marsicano-presentata-la-nuova-stima-di-popolazione-nellappennino-centrale
- Method: 34 technicians collected 608 hair samples in the field (plus 246 from regional monitoring networks); 70 distinct individuals were identified from DNA. The 81 is a statistical estimate, not a head count. [V] (same sources; also https://www.nnb.isprambiente.it/en/the-national-recovery-and-resilience-plan-nrrp-for-national-parks-and-marine-protected-areas-digitap-project/activities/collection-of-non-invasive-biological-samples-from-marsican-brown-bears-for-biomolecular-analyses-and-population-size-estimation)
- The previous estimate was about twelve years earlier, covering only the national park: roughly 45-69 animals. [V]
- Reported 2025 births: 16 cubs from 7 females (partial count, from montagna.tv). [V/?] https://www.montagna.tv/277283/quanti-sono-gli-orsi-marsicani-la-risposta-dal-monitoraggio-genetico/amp/
- Genetically isolated for roughly 500-ish years from other brown bears; very low genetic diversity. [B]
- Novelist note: a village of ~500 people lives alongside a *subspecies* of ~80 animals. The bear is nearly as rare as a named person in a small town.

### Body size
- Sources disagree. Wikipedia-style figures: males about 217 kg, females about 140 kg; tourism sites: autumn males can exceed 230 kg, females rarely above 140 kg. [?] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsican_brown_bear
- The most rigorous source found (University of Padua study of anaesthetised wild bears, 1990-2013) gives wild adult males averaging only about **158 kg** (versus about 188 kg for captive adult males), and notes that females gain weight steadily from post-hibernation to pre-hibernation. [V] https://research.unipd.it/handle/11577/2842506
- Safe phrasing for the novel: a big male in autumn might be 150-200+ kg; a female roughly 100-140 kg. Wild bears are lighter than captive ones.

### Autumn diet and hyperphagia
- Best data: Ciucci et al. 2014, *Journal of Mammalogy* (2,359 scats, 2006-2009). In fall, **hard mast (beechnuts, acorns) made up about 66.9% of the diet; fleshy fruits about 26.3%**. Year-to-year diet swings with how much hard mast the forest produced. [V] https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-mammalogy/volume-95/issue-3/13-MAMM-A-218/Seasonal-and-annual-variation-in-the-food-habits-of-Apennine/10.1644/13-MAMM-A-218.full
- The same study flagged a **declining trend in late-summer buckthorn (Rhamnus) berry use**, which the authors read as a habitat-management warning. [V]
- Apples, plums, pears, wild fruit: I found **no Marsican-specific evidence in this session** for apples/plums. They are well known locally as crop-raiding targets in orchards and are plausible, but treat as [B/?]. Sorbus (rowan, service trees), blackthorn plums, wild pears, and figs/grapes in the lower valleys are commonly cited. [B]
- Ants (Formica etc.) and other insects are a spring/summer item more than autumn. [B]
- Hyperphagia numbers: widely quoted figure of up to ~20,000 kcal/day for Alaskan/European brown bears in peak fall feeding, and fat gain of 30-40% of body mass. I could not verify these for Marsican bears. [B/?] Better to say "it eats for most of its waking hours".
- Marsican bears do hibernate, but lightly; pregnant females den (cubs born in winter, ~February per sources; tiny, under 500 g at birth). Males in mild years may stay active. [V for cubs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsican_brown_bear ; B for light hibernation]

### Behaviour: night, villages, mothers
- Adults are mostly nocturnal/crepuscular, except mothers with cubs or during mating. [V] (Wikipedia/tourism summary). Close to people they shift further toward night.
- Bears enter villages (Pescasseroli, Villavallelonga, Opi etc.) mostly at night/dawn to feed on orchards, garbage, chicken coops, beehives. Pescasseroli travel sources say dawn visits, mainly spring-early summer. [V, low-quality sources]
- Marsican bears are famously non-aggressive toward people; there is no recent record of fatal attacks (often called one of the most timid brown bears). [B/?] Worth confirming before stating as fact.
- Mothers with cubs: cubs stay with the mother roughly 1.5 years (sources differ on a shorter figure). Mating in late spring; a female may mate with several males, so a litter can have multiple fathers. [V/?] Sows with cubs avoid males (infanticide risk) and sometimes use human-modified areas as refuge. [B]
- **Amarena** (the famous female): shot dead by a 56-year-old resident of San Benedetto dei Marsi, just outside the park, August 2023. He said he fired "out of fear" after finding her on his property; later reports say he claimed self-defence. Investigation for cruel killing of animals (penalty 4 months-2 years). Her cubs were orphaned. [V] https://www.wantedinrome.com/news/italy-mourns-amarena-bear-shot-dead-abruzzo.html ; https://www.wionews.com/world/one-of-italys-last-remaining-marsican-brown-bear-shot-dead-out-of-fear-631735/amp
- **Juan Carrito** (Amarena's cub, ~4 years old): killed in January 2023 by a car on the state road SS17 near Castel di Sangro, close to a wildlife underpass. He had become famous for wandering into Roccaraso, breaking into a biscuit/pastry premises. He had been relocated/held in captivity at one point (Telegraph headline: "Captivity is just deserts for bear with taste for pastry", 2022). [V] https://www.ansa.it/english/news/general_news/2023/01/24/famed-foraging-bear-killed-by-car-in-abruzzo_1e5cb191-349b-4248-bfdb-629f3d8560bc.html ; https://www.agi.it/cronaca/news/2023-01-25/orso-juan-carrito-giusta-distanza-19799280/
- Narrative use: both famous bears were killed by *people's infrastructure* (a gun, a road). Italians reacted with national mourning.

### Senses
- Smell: widely repeated claim "2,100x human, ~7x a bloodhound" is **not traceable to a primary study**. Bear olfactory bulb is large; nasal cavity has large turbinate surface. Say "extraordinarily acute" rather than quoting a ratio. [V that it's untraceable: https://bearvault.com/how-well-can-bears-smell/ ; https://westernwildlife.org/?p=464]
- Hearing: second most important sense; small rounded ears but good detection at distance. [V, low-quality]
- Vision: not confirmed in session. Brown bears are generally described as having decent daytime vision, dichromatic colour vision, adapted to dim light (tapetum). [B]
- Bears are said to track a human from scent for long distances and to know a village by its smells (rotting fruit, chicken feed, honey, hot bread). [B]

### Local measures
- "Marsican Bear Smart Communities" (Salviamo l'Orso / Rewilding Europe / Fondation Segré and partners): bear-proof garbage bins, removal of fruit trees along streets and in towns, electric fences for hives, orchards, chicken coops (49 electric fences distributed in four corridors in 2018), metal doors on outbuildings. Second phase funded 2023. [V] https://www.fondationsegre.org/marsican-bear-smart-communities/ ; https://rewildingeurope.com/blog/strengthening-community-relations-aids-marsican-bear-recovery/ ; https://www.cinemaverde.org/post/we-made-everything-bear-proof-the-italian-village-that-learned-to-love-its-bears
- A named bear "Peppina" was known for raiding chickens, bees, orchards. [V]
- Bears and sheepdogs: Maremmano-Abruzzese dogs are bred to confront wolves and bears (see section 3). [V]

## 2. Ancient beech forests

### Facts
- **Val Cervara (PNALM)**: old-growth beech stand, about 100 ha (one abstract says ~50 ha) at **1,400-1,800 m**. Schirone et al. report trees up to ~500 years, "twice the maximum age usually reported for the species". Park-related sources claim specimens over 560 years, "the oldest beeches of the northern hemisphere". [V/?] https://www.peiresc.org/static/phytosocio/Schirone.htm ; https://blog.wownature.eu/en/the-most-ancient-forests-in-italy/ ; https://faggetevetuste.it/en/?p=1590
- Normal beech life span is ~250-300 years [B], so 500+ is double. Treeline-altitude beeches grow slowly, which helps longevity (slow growth = dense wood = resistance). [B]
- **UNESCO**: "Ancient and Primeval Beech Forests of the Carpathians and Other Regions of Europe" (World Heritage). Val Cervara and Selva Moricento are components of the PNALM cluster; a proposed extension would enlarge the Moricento core (a north-west addition of ~54 ha in Villavallelonga and Lecce nei Marsi). Italian beech forests joined the serial site in 2017; the property now has ~94 component parts in 18 countries. [V/?] https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6943/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_and_Primeval_Beech_Forests_of_the_Carpathians_and_Other_Regions_of_Europe . My search did NOT confirm the exact list of Italian components or the listing year per component; verify on UNESCO (document whc.unesco.org/document/181053).
- Beech ecology in late September: leaves are still mostly green with first yellowing; October brings gold then copper. Beechnuts (faggiole, in four-valved prickly husks) fall in September-October. [B]
- Mast years: beech mast (heavy seed years) irregular, every ~5-10 years; triggers include a warm summer the year before and are synchronized across a region. In a mast year bears, boar, dormice, jays and woodpigeons feast; in a failure year bears turn to crops and villages (this ties directly into the bear data above). [B]
- Beech biology details: smooth silver-grey bark (thin, easily sun-scalded); "diffuse-porous" wood with fine rings; shallow, wide root plate; beeches often form natural root grafts with neighbours. [B]
- Old beeches survive hollow: trees wall off decay (compartmentalisation; "CODIT" model by Alex Shigo), so the living cambium and outer sapwood carry water while the dead heartwood is eaten by fungi; a hollow tube is mechanically quite strong. The hollow becomes habitat for bats, owls, dormice, stag-beetle and saproxylic beetle larvae. [B]
- Rings: a core from a 500-year-old tree is often impossible because the heart is rotten, so age is estimated from outer rings plus diameter. [B/?]

### How trees sense (careful framing)
- **Mycorrhizal networks ("wood wide web")**: beech is an ectomycorrhizal species. Suzanne Simard's 1997 *Nature* experiment showed carbon isotopes moving between birch and Douglas fir. [B]
- **The critique (be honest in the book!)**: Karst, Jones & Hoeksema 2023 in *Nature Ecology & Evolution* ("Positive citation bias and overinterpreted results lead to misinformation on common mycorrhizal networks in forests") argue that popular claims go well beyond the evidence: (1) CMNs are widespread in forests, (2) they transfer resources that boost seedlings, (3) mature "mother trees" preferentially send resources and defence signals to kin. They judge (1) and (2) insufficiently supported and (3) to have **no peer-reviewed published evidence**. They analysed 593 papers on CMN structure and 1,083 on function, and found unsupported statements in citations rose to ~25% (structure) and almost 50% (function). [V] https://karstlab.ualberta.ca/?p=20 ; https://pubpeer.com/publications/970BFA6B9DA03A5BA1E7A1132BA64E
- A 2024 rebuttal in *Frontiers in Forests and Global Change* argues the Karst search was narrow and the selection of 18 studies subjective. [V] https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/forests-and-global-change/articles/10.3389/ffgc.2024.1512518/pdf
- Defensible middle line: fungi do connect roots; carbon can move through them; whether it matters to tree fitness, and whether trees "intend" anything, is unproven. A novelist who wants the tree chapter to be accurate should render the network as *a possibility the tree could not know about*, not as a conversation.
- **Drought clicks**: water-stressed wood emits ultrasonic clicks; a 2014 study tied each click to nucleation of a single cavitation bubble in the xylem (the water column snaps). Mechanism not fully settled (some low-amplitude emissions may come from respiration/cambial growth). The best-known data are from pines, oaks, grapevine, not beech specifically. [V] https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01060558 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4233727
- Light, gravity, touch: trees bend toward light (phototropism, blue-light photoreceptors), sense gravity with starch-heavy statoliths, and respond to mechanical stress/wind by thickening trunks (thigmomorphogenesis). [B]
- Trees do not "feel" a storm as a nervous system would, but turgor, hydraulic tension and wind sway change instantly. A beech in a late-September thunderstorm is still in full leaf and catches wind like a sail. [B]

## 3. Dog olfaction and the local dogs

- **Receptors**: ~300 million olfactory receptors in dogs vs ~5-6 million in humans is the standard popular figure; sources vary widely (125 to 300+ million depending on breed). Also: dogs have ~800+ functional olfactory receptor genes vs ~400 in humans [B]. [V/?] https://www.k9md.org.nz/the-wonder-of-a-dog-s-nose
- **Horowitz**: in *Inside of a Dog* she gives the sugar-in-a-million-gallons analogy (a teaspoon of sugar in a million gallons of water; also the "swimming pool" variants circulate). Her larger idea: dogs perceive time partly through smell, because odour fades; a smell at a spot carries the *age* of the visit; and incoming air gives hints of the *near future* (who is coming). The teaspoon analogy is verified as quoted; the decay-as-time reading is her argument, not a lab-proven finding. [V/?] https://www.mantrailinguk.com/post/oldies-but-goldies
- **Trailing vs air scenting**: trailing dogs follow the shed skin cells ("rafts") and the age gradient of the track, and detect direction of travel by subtle change in scent age; air-scent dogs work the plume of a person in the wind. [V] (same Mantrail UK page). Italians call trailing dogs *cani molecolari*.
- **Direction from a few steps**: Hepper & Wells 2005 (*Chemical Senses*) found dogs can tell the direction of a track from only a handful of footsteps (I recall ~3-5). [B/? check]
- **Rain/running water**: I found no good source in this session. General SAR knowledge: heavy rain and running water dilute and move particles, can wash the trail off hard surfaces, and wind scatters raft plumes; but humid, cool, still air actually *holds* scent close to the ground, and a trail under dense leaf litter or tree canopy can persist surprisingly long. Dogs may follow a stream edge, lose the scent in the water, and have to cast on both banks. [B]
- **Sniffing mechanics**: exhaled air leaves through the side slits of the nose, creating swirling turbulence that helps draw new odour in at the centre of each nostril (Settles / Craven et al., Penn State). A dog sniffs ~5-10 times per second when working. Only a minor fraction of each breath is routed to the olfactory region (a 12% figure circulates on secondary sites - [?]). [V/?] https://www.medicaldetectiondogs.org.uk/sniffing ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/20007171
- **Vomeronasal organ**: in the roof of the mouth, a small duct to the nasal cavity; detects pheromones and chemical messages (reproduction, emotional state). That's why dogs sometimes chatter their teeth or lift the lip (flehmen-like). [V/B] https://www.k9md.org.nz/the-wonder-of-a-dog-s-nose
- **What a dog smells in a human**: Wilson et al. 2022 (*PLOS ONE*): four dogs distinguished breath/sweat samples from people before vs after a stressful mental-arithmetic task with **93.75% accuracy** (range 90-96.88%). Fear and stress change the odour bouquet. [V] https://phys.org/news/2022-09-dogs-stressed.html ; https://www.sci.news/biology/dog-human-stress-11241.html
- Sensory image for a dog chapter: the boy's trail smells of *age* in layers; his mother's panic on the trail has a sour edge; the dog knows the father's hours-old route before he arrives. [B, imaginative]

### Maremmano-Abruzzese sheepdog
- Large white livestock-guardian dog, first described in Roman literature "over 2,000 years ago" (loose claim); linked to transhumance, flocks going to the Adriatic plain in winter and the high Apennines in summer. Males ~65-73 cm; thick white coat; formally shown in Brescia in 1952. Defends against wolves and bears; guards silently, regards territory as its own; lies still by day, patrols at night. Poor fit for city/family life. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maremmano-Abruzzese_Sheepdog ; https://www.zooplus.co.uk/magazine/dog/dog-breeds/maremma-abruzzese-sheepdog-maremmano
- One source's claim that the breed descends from the Tibetan Mastiff is doubtful. [?]
- In Abruzzo the shepherd dogs wear spiked iron collars against wolves (often called *vreccale* in local usage - exact word/spelling uncertain). [B/?]
- Livestock-guardian dogs do not "protect" a child by training, but they are alert to anything moving at night and bark at anything near the flock. A white dog is visible in dark to humans. [B]

## 4. Crows and other corvids

- **Present in Abruzzo/PNALM**: hooded crow *Corvus cornix* (the grey-bodied "cornacchia grigia"; the common village crow of central and southern Italy), carrion crow rarer, jackdaw *Corvus monedula*, common raven *Corvus corax* (breeds on cliffs; recovering in the Apennines), Alpine chough *Pyrrhocorax graculus* and red-billed chough *P. pyrrhocorax* (high limestone, e.g. Majella, Gran Sasso), Eurasian jay *Garrulus glandarius* (acorn/beech cacher), magpie *Pica pica*. [B - check against local checklists]
- **Marzluff face memory**: University of Washington experiments with rubber masks (a "dangerous" mask worn by researchers who trapped and banded crows; a neutral mask for control). Crows kept scolding the dangerous mask for at least five years after only seven original crows had been trapped, and the behaviour spread to ~1.2 km from the site through observation and offspring. They scold even when the person is seen for only a couple of hours twice a year. Similar face recognition in magpies (South Korea) and mockingbirds (Florida). [V] https://nwf.org/Magazines/National-Wildlife/2013/DecJan/Animals/Crows-Recognizing-Faces ; https://www.hcn.org/blogs/goat/the-crow-knows-your-nose/
- Brain imaging in Marzluff's work showed amygdala-linked and motor circuits lighting up for dangerous faces (2012 PNAS). [B]
- **Funerals**: Swift & Marzluff 2015: crows gather and scold around dead crows - they appear to be *learning about danger*, not mourning (the "funeral" is a folk reading). [V that the "funeral" idea is popular folklore; B for Swift & Marzluff conclusion] https://blainecountyjournal.com/story/2023/11/22/news/crows-clever-opportunistic-and-prone-to-hold-grudges-against-certain-humans/9118.html
- **Mobbing**: crows converge on owls, hawks, cats, and sometimes humans, with harsh calls recruiting neighbours, dive-bombing; they mob perched owls by day (an owl found at dusk is a target). [B]
- **Roosts**: in autumn/winter, hooded crows and jackdaws gather in communal roosts of dozens to thousands at dusk, in tall trees; birds stream in from fields from late afternoon; roost noise peaks at dusk then goes quiet. [B]
- **Vision**: birds are tetrachromats with a UV-sensitive cone class; corvid plumage that looks black to us has UV and blue iridescent structure; eyes are on the sides with a large visual field; poor night vision (they are diurnal and are not flying in dark). [B] I found no source in the session for corvid-specific UV vision.
- **Voice**: wide repertoire including caws, rattles, "toy-trumpet" sounds, mimicry in captivity; the raven's deep croak and "tok" calls carry across valleys; alpine choughs give a rippling "chreeah". [B]
- Night angle: at night the crow is silent, huddled, and afraid of owls; a late-night caw means disturbance (flashlights, drones, helicopter). [B]

## 5. Honeybees at night, storms, and folk custom

- **Italian bee**: *Apis mellifera ligustica* - the yellow/leather-coloured Ligurian bee, gentle, prolific, widely exported. [B]
- **Brood temperature**: brood nest held at about **35 °C** (ideal 32-36 °C); sustained <32 °C risks malformed wings/legs/abdomens, >36 °C is also harmful. [V] https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2023.1119452/pdf ; https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/206/23/4217/1549296/4217.pdf
- **Fanning**: bees fan wings at the entrance to cool and ventilate the hive; they carry water and fan over it for evaporative cooling. [V] (Mississippi extension newsletter, Frontiers). In cool September nights the problem is the opposite: the cluster tightens and bees shiver flight muscles to generate heat while keeping the brood warm. [B]
- **Sleep**: bees sleep (Walter Kaiser's work): drooping antennae, relaxed posture, lowered body temperature; foragers sleep more at night in the hive; older foragers sleep longer. Not verified in session. [B]
- **Electric fields**: Clarke, Whitney, Sutton & Robert, *Science* 2013 (note: *Science*, not PNAS): **bumblebees** detect floral electric fields and learn them (81% accuracy choosing charged flowers). Sutton et al. 2016 *PNAS*: it's the mechanosensory hairs that detect the field. **Honeybees** (Greggers et al. 2013, *PLOS ONE*) can detect weak electric fields and perceive electrostatic charge on dancing bees. Bee bodies carry a positive charge from flight friction; the atmosphere has a standing electric field that rises before thunderstorms. [V for Clarke/Sutton/Greggers existence: https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2013/9163.html ; https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2016/may/dancing-hairs-alert-bees.html ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4932987 ; B for the storm link]
- **Waggle dance**: angle from vertical on the comb = direction of food relative to the sun's azimuth; duration of the waggle run ~1 second per km (roughly); on a vertical comb in the dark hive. Dances are followed by bees touching the dancer's body in the dark. [B]
- **Polarised light / navigation**: bees read the polarisation pattern of the sky via a special dorsal rim area of the eye, so they can orient when the sun is hidden; ocelli (three simple eyes) help in dim light. Magnetoreception: bees contain magnetite and can be affected by magnetic fields. [B]
- **Before a storm**: foragers rush home, entrances crowd, guard bees are more defensive, and beekeepers say bees "know" a storm is coming from pressure, humidity and field changes. This is anecdotal/likely; I found no rigorous source in session. [B/?]
- **Telling the bees**: the custom (reporting deaths, births, marriages, departures to the hives; draping hives in black; knock once or three times and speak) is documented across England, Ireland, Wales, Germany, the Netherlands, France, Switzerland, Bohemia, and the Basque Country (formula "nagusia hil da" = "the master has died"). Failure to tell → the bees die or leave. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telling_the_bees ; https://labayru.eus/en/basque-ethnography-at-a-glance/telling-the-bees ; **No Italian-specific evidence found in this session.** Don't state it as an Italian tradition; a character can bring it from a foreign wife or a book, or the author can invent a local variant knowingly. [?]
- Bees and bears: bears wreck apiaries; local beekeepers use electric fences. [V - bear measures]

## 6. Bats in Abruzzo

- **Species recorded in PNALM/Abruzzo** (from a protected-area list): greater horseshoe *Rhinolophus ferrumequinum*, lesser horseshoe *R. hipposideros*, greater mouse-eared *Myotis myotis*, Brandt's *M. brandtii*, barbastelle *Barbastella barbastellus*, Schreiber's bat *Miniopterus schreibersii*. Also *Rhinolophus euryale* confirmed in the Stiffe karst cave (L'Aquila): 14 of 34 bats captured were this species, evidence of a hibernation site. [V] https://secemu.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Salari_et_al_2015.pdf ; https://checklist.pensoft.net/article_preview.php?id=185832
- Likely in villages [B, check]: common pipistrelle *Pipistrellus pipistrellus*, Kuhl's pipistrelle *P. kuhlii*, Savi's pipistrelle *Hypsugo savii*, noctule *Nyctalus noctula*, brown long-eared *Plecotus auritus* / grey long-eared, and the **European free-tailed bat *Tadarida teniotis*** whose low call (about 10-15 kHz) is audible to some humans and which nests in cliff crevices and old buildings. [B]
- **Echolocation frequencies**: lesser horseshoe constant-frequency call about **108-118 kHz** (~110 kHz) [V: https://www.dartington.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/bat-survey-2.pdf]; greater horseshoe ~80 kHz; common pipistrelle ~45 kHz, soprano ~55 kHz; Savi's ~32-35 kHz; noctule ~20-25 kHz. [B]
- **Horseshoe bat echolocation**: long constant-frequency calls with Doppler-shift compensation: the bat lowers its call as it flies so the returning echo sits at a fixed frequency; the wing beat modulates the echo, letting the bat detect a fluttering moth against clutter. A 2026 PNAS press release describes horseshoe bats' long CF ultrasonic calls. [V/B] https://uni-tuebingen.de/ (press release PDF, 2026-06-23)
- **What echolocation "renders"**: shape, distance (echo delay: ~6 ms per metre round trip is a good approximate rule), speed (Doppler), texture (spectral colour of echoes: fur, leaf, water), and the flutter of insect wings. Water is a smooth mirror - bats drink by skimming, and the sound reflects away from a calm surface as if it were a floor. [B]
- **Rain**: raindrop noise and wet surfaces mask echoes and attenuate high frequencies; bats forage less on rainy nights, and many stay in their roost. Light rain/drizzle may still see activity. [V: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2022.950951/pdf ; B for "light rain"]
- **September swarming**: in autumn many European bats gather at cave mouths and hibernacula (late summer to autumn, "swarming") for mating and to explore hibernation sites; a 2017 study found *no consistent evidence* of autumn swarming in lesser horseshoes (their peak was spring). [V] https://secemu.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Thomas_et_al_2017_Endnote.txt (swarming strongly documented in Myotis, Plecotus, Barbastella - B)
- **Nagel (1974), "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"** (*The Philosophical Review*): an organism has conscious experience if and only if there is something it is like to be that organism; bat sonar is not like any sense we have, so even if we imagine flying and hanging upside down we imagine ourselves behaving like bats, not what it is like *for the bat*; subjective facts cannot be captured by objective physical description; yet we should not doubt the bat has experience. Nagel picked bats because they are mammals (so likely have experience) yet alien in sense. [B - widely known; not re-verified]
- **Roosts** in caves, old houses, hermitages: rock hermitages of the Majella (see section 7) are plausible roosts for horseshoe bats - the bats' small droppings (guano) in stone cells. [B/imaginative]

## 7. Abruzzo in WWII, winter 1943-44

- **Context**: After the 8 September 1943 armistice, Germany occupied central Italy. The Gustav (Winter) Line ran across Abruzzo along the Sangro and through Cassino; the front stalled over winter. Germans evacuated or deported villagers from the front zone, destroyed villages (Roccaraso was razed), requisitioned food and livestock. [B for overall; V for some details below]
- **Pietransieri massacre (21 November 1943)** in Roccaraso: Wehrmacht soldiers killed ~125-128 civilians (mostly women, children, elderly) after an evacuation order some had refused; people were penned in farm buildings, shot, and the buildings dynamited. Sources: 125 (Wikipedia list), 128 (ANPI). No serious investigation until the 1990s. A memorial stands at Limmari; a torchlit procession on 20 November. [V] https://www.ns-taeter-italien.org/en/massacres/pietransieri ; https://www.anpi.it/patria-indipendente/media/uploads/patria/2013/malvestuto_storiamemoria_pietransieri_dicembre_2012.pdf ; https://liberationroute.com/pois/1204/war-memorial-in-limmari ; https://www.avvenire.it/agora/cultura/pietransieri-80-anni-fa-la-strage-senza-prescrizione_71073
- **Brigata Maiella**: partisan formation set up December 1943, disbanded at Brisighella on 15 July 1945; fought with the Allies up the peninsula; the only partisan unit awarded the Gold Medal of Military Valour. Founded by Ettore Troilo [B]. [V] https://www.fondazionebrigatamaiella.it/media/download/196/a444b6cdb34947c7f707e1b8285f98105841d74b ; https://archivio.unita.news/assets/main/2014/02/01/page_019.pdf
- **Sentiero della Libertà**: escape route from the Fonte d'Amore POW camp at Sulmona over the Majella to Allied lines (Casoli area). Walkers crossed at night, hid by day; the Guado di Coccia pass was patrolled by Germans; also used by young Italians refusing the Salò republic, Jews, and political opponents. Carlo Azeglio Ciampi (later President) crossed in March 1944 and wrote of it. Villagers fed and sheltered prisoners. [V] https://www.sportoutdoor24.it/?p=665314 . Length/elevation: unverified. [?]
- **Food**: no verified source in this session. Plausible [B]: polenta/maize bread (*pane di granturco*), chestnuts (more a Lazio/Molise item; less in high Abruzzo), beans, lentils, chickpeas, farro, wild greens (cicoria), snails, cheese and ricotta where flocks survived, hidden grain, acorn bread in desperation. Salt scarcity. Villagers hid food in caves and walls.
- **Hiding places**: caves, shepherd huts (*cottura*/*tholos* dry-stone huts), snow-bound huts, and rock hermitages. [B]
- **Eremi of the Majella (Celestine V)**:
  - **Eremo di Santo Spirito a Majella** (Roccamorice): the largest and best-known; nestled in the folds of a jagged rock face, buildings stepping across various heights as if set into the stone. Includes church, guest house, cells; a *Scala Santa* (holy stairs) leads to the Maddalena oratory and two covered balconies. Pietro da Morrone (later Celestine V) found it ruined and renovated it from 1246 and made it mother-house of his congregation in 1263 (dates vary by source). The central cell below the church was his prayer cell. [V] https://borgosanmartino.eu/en/11-excursions/54-hermitage-of-holy-spirit ; https://www.finestresullarte.info/en/travel/majella-park-what-to-see-10-stops-among-nature-history-art
  - **Eremo di San Bartolomeo in Legio** (Roccamorice): on a rock spur ~50 m high at ~700 m; a chapel and two rock-cut rooms; four stairways cut into rock (north: 30 steps; south: longer and irregular); a basin on the balcony collects rainwater; associated with Pietro da Morrone around 1250, again c.1274 after Lyon. [V] https://www.visititaly.eu/art-and-culture/the-thousand-year-old-charm-of-the-hermitage-of-san-bartolomeo-in-legio ; https://museionline.info/abruzzo-musei-monumenti/eremo-di-san-bartolomeo-in-legio
  - Celestine V (Pietro da Morrone) was elected pope in 1294 (while at Sant'Onofrio on the Morrone above Sulmona) and abdicated after five months - Dante's "colui che fece per viltade il gran rifiuto" is usually read as him. [B]
- Note for the novelist: Majella is a different massif from PNALM; a fictional valley can borrow hermitage imagery without being in the Majella (geography is "near").

## 8. The river and mountain streams

- **Sangro**: about 122 km long, basin ~1,545 km², rises near Pescasseroli in Abruzzo National Park (Wikipedia names the Monte Morrone del Diavolo/Passo del Diavolo area), flows southeast past Opi and Villetta Barrea into the artificial **Lago di Barrea** (about 2.25 km²), then out toward the Adriatic. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangro ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Barrea
- Britannica notes that deforestation of the upper slopes means floods and landslides occur frequently during spring and autumn rains. [V] https://www.britannica.com/place/Sangro-River
- **Flash-flood rise times**: no good Apennine-specific number found. General small-catchment behaviour [B]: in steep limestone/flysch catchments a thunderstorm of 40-60 mm/h can raise a stream from ankle-deep to waist-deep or more in under an hour (often 15-60 minutes of lag), arriving as a pulse; the water turns brown, then loud; boulders knock together underwater ("rolling bedload" is audible); debris (branches, bark, leaves) is carried. Comparable Italian events: Misa river at Senigallia, September 2022, overtopped the town centre in a night (10+ deaths) [V: https://www.ksat.com/news/world/2022/09/16/floods-in-italy-kill-10-survivors-plucked-from-roofs-trees/]. Use as context, not as a measurement for your valley.
- **What a river carries**: gravel and cobble bedload, leaves and beech husks, dissolved calcium carbonate (limestone water), insect larvae, trout (Mediterranean/Apennine brown trout, *Salmo ghigii*? - [?]), otter signs (the Apennines have otters, *Lutra lutra*), sediment, pollen, flotsam from villages (plastic, shoes). [B]
- **Karst springs**: limestone swallows rain into fissures and returns it at springs cold (about 7-10 °C year-round) and clear; after storms they pulse, clouded with sediment; dry gorges sometimes run only after storms. [B] Large Abruzzo karst springs feed the Pescara and Tirino etc. [B/?]
- Temperature: cold mountain water in September; a child in it would get cold quickly. [B]

## 9. Elder-care monitoring devices and dementia

### What real systems do (as of ~2025-2026)
- **Radar and non-camera fall detection**: ceiling or wall radar sensors read body position and trajectory, trained on tens of thousands of positions, learn "exclusion zones" such as a rocking chair; privacy-friendly (no images). Research uses deep learning (CNN/RNN) on radar spectrograms. [V] https://www.essence-grp.com/blog/the-future-of-senior-care-harnessing-the-power-of-machine-learning-for-a-safer-more-independent-life/ ; https://profiles.arizona.edu/person/mhector
- **Wandering detection**: ambient sensors learn routines and flag anomalies; a 2025-2027 Swedish (Mälardalen University) project is building radar-based AI for wandering indicators; smart-floor systems (Wi-Fi + floor sensors) track gait and wandering. [V] https://www.es.mdu.se/projects/647-SDWB ; https://content.iospress.com/articles/journal-of-intelligent-and-fuzzy-systems/ifs169903
- **Review**: a 2025 *JMIR Aging* systematic review of remote monitoring for Alzheimer's and related dementias (JMIR Aging 2025;8:e69175) emphasises non-camera, privacy-respecting hardware. [V] https://blog.jmir.org/the-connected-home-how-smart-tech-is-redefining-safety-for-independent-older-adults
- **Voice assistants**: prototypes combine voice reminders, fall detection, GPS and caregiver apps. These are prototypes, not validated products. [V]
- Commercial devices commonly found [B]: pendant alarms with fall detection, GPS watches, door/bed-exit sensors, medication dispensers, smart speakers with routines, companion robots (ElliQ) for loneliness. Amazon's "Alexa Together" caregiver service existed but I believe it was discontinued; unverified. [B/?]
- **Italian context**: *telesoccorso/teleassistenza* (a button pendant linked to a call centre) is the usual rural service; coverage in mountain villages is patchy because of poor mobile/data signal. [B/?]
- **What such a system "knows"** (useful for an AI-voice chapter): time-stamped door events, bed occupancy, motion, a missing step count, voice tone, last conversation, battery level, no camera. It speaks only when asked and may log everything locally. An honest AI voice chapter would be constrained: it senses almost nothing of the outdoors, only the absence of expected signals. [B, imaginative]

### Dementia: time and talk
- **Time-shifting** ("living in the past"): in Alzheimer's/dementia, recent memory fails first while older memories stay, so a person may live in an earlier decade, look for dead relatives, or "collect the children from school" (Ribot gradient). A training guide cites a person feeling they must collect their children as a classic case for validation. [V/B] https://www.atrainceu.com/node/1472
- **Validation therapy** (Naomi Feil, 1960s onward): accept the emotional reality of what the person says rather than correcting; concerns feelings, not cognition. **Reality orientation**: gently and repeatedly restating date, place, circumstances; generally seen as more useful in early stages and potentially distressing in later stages (catastrophic reactions). The Cochrane review found **insufficient evidence** that validation therapy works, and the method has been criticised. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Validation_therapy ; https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD001394/full/nl
- **Therapeutic deception**: caregivers avoid re-delivering painful facts (e.g. "your husband died") because the person relives the grief each time. [V]
- **Sundowning**: increased confusion and agitation in late afternoon/evening; managed by reducing stimulation and increasing light. Causes are debated (circadian disruption, fatigue, low light). [V] https://www.atrainceu.com/node/4474
- Narrative use: a grandmother with dementia in a village at dusk may sincerely tell the searchers a child is "already home", or narrate events from 1943 as if now; both are consistent with real presentation. Beware turning her into a magical oracle.

## 10. Village life, search procedures, food, weather, dialect

### Village (2020s)
- Abruzzo has ~305 comuni; about 250 are "small" (under 5,000 inhabitants), roughly 82% of comuni but ~26% of population (2019 thesis figure); ageing index in small comuni exceeds the national average; ISTAT is building a demographic-desertification index for Abruzzo (2025). The whole province of L'Aquila is classified largely as an internal area. [V] https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/a81cb05d-37b2-616b-e053-1605fe0a889a/2019_04_Pedrazzini_DeSantis_Olivo_01.pdf ; https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/02-SLIDE-CARULLI-DI-SPALATRO.pdf ; https://www.openpolis.it/osservatorio-abruzzo
- I did NOT find a verified average age for a typical mountain comune. Typical figure for small Apennine comuni is mean age ~50-55 [B/?]. Pescasseroli (the PNALM "capital") has ~2,000 residents [B/?]; smaller PNALM villages (Opi, Civitella Alfedena, Barrea) are a few hundred to ~800.
- Features [B]: the bar as the village's central organ (coffee at 6, grappa, card games *scopa/briscola/tressette*), the church with a bell tower and a clock that sometimes strikes twice (the "double stroke" habit where the hour is struck, then repeated after a pause), summer/Ferragosto return of emigrants (the village swells in August and empties by late September), shuttered houses, a single shop, a pharmacy visit by van, a *Carabinieri* station possibly staffed only part-time. In late September most holidaymakers are gone - the night is quiet.
- Bells: Italian church bells signal events; a *campana a martello* (rapid hammering) historically meant alarm/fire/flood. [B/?]

### Search for a missing child (Italy)
- The **Prefettura** (provincial prefect) coordinates; the Carabinieri activate the provincial plan for missing persons ("piano provinciale per la ricerca persone scomparse") — there is no waiting period before searching for a minor. [V for coordination and Carabinieri activation: https://www.ilgiorno.it/brescia/cronaca/bambina-scomparsa-serle-64da61e4 ; https://www.lanazione.it/pistoia/cronaca/allarme-per-un-bimbo-ricerche-f8b3d6cd ; B for 'no waiting period']
- Forces: Vigili del Fuoco (including mountain and canine units), CNSAS (Soccorso Alpino e Speleologico), Protezione Civile volunteers, Carabinieri, Polizia, Guardia di Finanza, Forestali (Carabinieri Forestali), sometimes park rangers. Serle (Brescia, autistic girl): 265 operators on one day with dogs, helicopters, drones; the Prefecture's order: "search for a living person". [V] https://www.ilgiorno.it/brescia/cronaca/bambina-scomparsa-serle-64da61e4
- Tools: *cani molecolari* (trailing dogs); thermal-imaging drones (found a boy at night in a South Tyrol case, Anterselva/Chiusa); helicopter (in Campobasso a five-year-old girl was found in a ravine by a State Police helicopter with Soccorso Alpino after a night of fruitless ground searching). Search results are not guaranteed: Serle ran for days. [V] https://www.montagna.tv/46966/tredicenne-sparisce-nei-boschi-di-anterselva-ricerche-a-tappeto/amp/ ; https://www.ladige.it/attualita/bimbo-di-due-anni-scomparso-nei-boschi-notte-di-ricerche-senza-esito-l4ud0i5m
- Timings: no official figures found. General practice [B]: first 2-3 hours are volunteers and Carabinieri; dog teams and Vigili del Fuoco arrive in 1-3 hours depending on distance; helicopter and drones depend on weather, light, and thunderstorms (rain, low cloud, and lightning ground helicopters, thermal drones lose range in rain). Thunderstorm over a rugged valley can ground aircraft and drown scent — a believable cruelty for the plot. [B]
- A hypothermia point: a 6-year-old in a thin sweater at 1,000 m in late September with 5-6 °C night and rain is at real risk of hypothermia over several hours. [B]

### Weather (Pescasseroli as the nearest datum, ~1,167 m [B])
- Wikipedia climate table: mean daily minimum ~**6.4 °C in September, ~3.2 °C in October**; record lows around -5.9 °C (September) and -8.0 °C (October). Another site says September minimum ~3 °C (sources conflict). [V/?] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pescasseroli ; https://nomadseason.com/weather/italy/abruzzo/pescasseroli-september.html
- So at ~1,000 m on a late-September night, 5-9 °C is typical, frost possible on a clear night, thunderstorms still frequent in this season (Civil Protection orange/yellow alerts). [B]

### Food in September [B unless noted]
- Porcini and other mushrooms (in season; foraging requires a permit in some areas), apples (old local varieties), pears, plums, grapes and the Montepulciano d'Abruzzo harvest (late September-October at lower elevations), walnuts, figs at lower altitudes, early chestnuts. Saffron of Navelli/L'Aquila blooms late October-November (not September). Lentils of Santo Stefano di Sessanio. *Arrosticini* (sheep skewers) year-round; *pecora alla cottora/callara*; *sagne e fagioli*; *pallotte cacio e ova*; pecorino and ricotta; *ventricina* salami; *scrippelle 'mbusse*; *pizza di granone*. honey (acacia, millefiori) from local hives. [B]

### Dialect (ALL uncertain - check with a native Abruzzese speaker; dialects change from town to town)
- Source note: Di Pietrantonio's Abruzzese prose confirms that the dialects of Abruzzo vary markedly from one town to the next and local idioms are fading with the elderly. [V] https://lespresso.it/c/idee/2019/8/20/labruzzo-di-donatella-di-pietrantonio-il-cibo-e-parte-della-mia-identita/44301
- *Nonna* in many areas is simply *nonna*, or *nonne*/*nonnà* in vocative [?].
- *Fije mì* / *fijje* = "my son/my child" (from *figlio*); *fijjarelle*/*fijiarelle* diminutive = little child [?].
- *Citte!* (or *citto*) = "hush/quiet" and in some places little boy [?].
- *Mo'* = "now"; *jamme* = "let's go" (also Neapolitan); *statte bbone* = "take care/stay well"; *bbelle de nonna* = "grandma's beauty" (affectionate; the exact local form is a guess) [?].
- *Core* is "heart"; a grandmother's "*core mì*" (my heart) is plausible, widely Italian rather than only Abruzzese [?].
- Typical Abruzzese traits: final vowels reduced to schwa; *ll* → *jj*/*dd* in some places; *-nd-* → *-nn-* (*quanne* = quando); *lu*/*la* articles (*lu cane*). [B/?]

## Most evocative details (25)

1. The 2025 census counted **81 Marsican bears (73-88)**, identified from 608 hair samples snagged on wire — the whole subspecies could sit in a village hall. [V]
2. A Marsican bear's autumn diet is **~67% hard mast (beechnuts, acorns)** and ~26% fleshy fruit; a beech failure year sends bears into the village orchards. [V]
3. **Amarena** was shot "out of fear" on a man's property; her cub **Juan Carrito** died on a road he had learned to walk. Two famous bears, both killed by human infrastructure. [V]
4. Bear-proof bins, metal doors, and electric fences are the "architecture of coexistence" — a village that rebuilt itself around an animal. [V]
5. **Val Cervara beeches** reach 500-560 years at 1,400-1,800 m; a tree that was a seedling in the early 1500s, around the time of Columbus or just after. [V/?]
6. Beeches survive **hollow**: they wall off the rot (compartmentalisation) so the living ring carries water around a dead core. [B]
7. Water-stressed wood emits **ultrasonic clicks** — each one a single cavitation bubble in a xylem tube — the sound of a tree drinking too little. [V]
8. The "wood wide web" is **contested**: Karst et al. 2023 found no published evidence for mother trees sending kin resources and defence signals; honesty makes the tree chapter stronger. [V]
9. A dog's trail is a **stack of ages**: the nose reads direction from the fading gradient between footsteps; Horowitz argues smell is how dogs *feel time*. [V/?]
10. Dogs separated **stress sweat/breath** from calm samples with 93.75% accuracy (Wilson 2022). A dog can smell the searcher's fear. [V]
11. A dog's exhalation leaves through **side slits** in the nose and the swirl helps pull new scent in. [V]
12. Maremmano-Abruzzese dogs **lie still by day and patrol by night**; white, silent, they confront wolves and bears. [V]
13. Crows kept scolding a researcher's **rubber mask for at least five years**; seven trapped crows taught their young and neighbours up to 1.2 km away. [V]
14. The crow "funeral" is a folk reading; the gathering is **learning about danger**, possibly — a nice reversal for a crow chapter. [V/B]
15. Honeybees hold the brood nest at **~35 °C**; below 32 °C for long and wings and legs form wrong. [V]
16. Bees carry a static charge and can **sense electric fields** (bumblebee hair work: Clarke 2013, Sutton 2016; honeybees: Greggers 2013); the electrical atmosphere before a thunderstorm is something a hive might register. [V; link to storm = B/?]
17. "Telling the bees" is documented across Western Europe and the Basque Country, but **no Italian record found**: a character can import it. [V]
18. Lesser horseshoe bats call at about **110 kHz**, a constant tone whose Doppler shift they compensate for; the **free-tailed bat** is the one whose call a human might actually hear. [V/B]
19. Rain **masks the echoes** bats live by; a thunderstorm night is a blind night for bats. [V]
20. **Pietransieri, 21 Nov 1943**: ~128 civilians, mostly women, children and elderly, shot in farm buildings which were then dynamited; no serious inquiry until the 1990s. [V]
21. The **Sentiero della Libertà** from the Sulmona POW camp: crossings at night, hiding by day, the Guado di Coccia pass watched by Germans; future President Ciampi walked it in March 1944. [V]
22. At **San Bartolomeo in Legio** a basin on the rock balcony collects rainwater, and four stone stairways have been cut into the cliff, the shortest 30 steps; hermits lived in rock-cut rooms. [V]
23. The **Sangro** rises near Pescasseroli, runs 122 km, and is dammed at Lago di Barrea; its slopes are so deforested that autumn rains produce floods. [V]
24. Radar-based elder monitors **see no faces and no images**: they know a body's position, a fall, an absence of expected steps. An AI voice in a house knows the world only through gaps. [V]
25. The Cochrane review could find **no sufficient evidence** that Feil's validation therapy works — yet it remains humane practice; a grandmother who tells the searchers the child is already home is not necessarily wrong about *feeling*. [V]
